\chapter{The number theory of the sieve and its choice}\label{chap:sieve-numbers}

The main search of \jpoints{} runs over the triples $(a, b, c)$ of the first three coordinates of
the Kummer surface (Chapter~\ref{chap:theorems}) and drops those over which no point can lie, by
tests of three kinds: in the three stages of the sieve, the class of the triple modulo each of the
sieving primes, odd primes of the table \texttt{prime[]}; on the rows, the class of the triple
modulo~$64$, the condition at~$2$; and for a monic quintic, the first coordinates that are not
squares. This chapter gives the number theory behind these tests, the tables in which the program
keeps them, and the choice of the sieve: which primes the stages use, and which of the box, the tube
and the real region the search runs over. A cost model in floating-point arithmetic makes the
choice, and no proof evaluates it: what the proofs show is that every choice it can make is sound,
so that the choice changes the work of the search but not its output
(Section~\ref{sec:sieveOutput}).

Throughout, $\mathit{cs}$ is the list of the coefficients of the curve searched, $f_k$ its entry~$k$
($0$ beyond the list), $f = \sum_k f_k x^k$ of degree $\delta \in \{5, 6\}$, and
$F(x, z) = \sum_{k \le 6} f_k x^k z^{6 - k}$ the binary sextic form, so that
$F(x, z) = z^6 f(x / z)$; $K = K_2 d^2 + K_1 d + K_0$ is the Kummer equation over $(a, b, c)$
and~$A_2$, $B_2$ are the polynomials of the lifting test (Definitions~\ref{def:kummer}
and~\ref{def:auxA2}). For an integer~$x$ and $p > 0$, $x \bmod p$ is the remainder in~$[0, p)$.

\section{The classes modulo an odd prime}\label{sec:sieveClasses}

A point of the Jacobian whose image on the Kummer surface is $(a : b : c : d)$ with $a \ne 0$ has
the Mumford representation $(x^2 - (b/a) x + c/a,\ v_1 x + v_0)$: the divisor is cut out by
$y = v_1 x + v_0$ on the roots of the quadratic, so that~$f$ is congruent to $(v_1 x + v_0)^2$
modulo the quadratic. Reduced modulo an odd prime $p \nmid a$, the remainder of~$f$ modulo
$X^2 - b' X + c'$, with $b' \equiv b/a$ and $c' \equiv c/a \bmod p$, is therefore the remainder of a
square, and that is what \texttt{point\_on\_j} tests (\csource{j-points.c}{762}{777}). For
$p \mid a$ and $p \nmid b$ the divisor reduces to a point at infinity plus a point with
$x \equiv c/b$, which the tables of the points at infinity and of the values of~$f$ that are squares
test; for $p \mid a$ and $p \mid b$ the tests admit every class.

The tests read the inverses that \texttt{invert} computes (\csource{j-points.c}{708}{716}) and the
square roots that \texttt{init\_squares} records (\csource{j-points.c}{726}{738}). The definitions
of this section compute what the C code computes, in exact arithmetic, with the tables replaced by
the functions that fill them; Section~\ref{sec:sieveTables} shows that the tables of the program are
these references.

\begin{definition}[Inverses and squares modulo a prime]\label{def:refIsSquare}
  \lean{JPoints.refInverse, JPoints.refSquareRoot, JPoints.refIsSquare, JPoints.refHasInfinity, JPoints.refIsFSquare}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:auxA2}
  For natural numbers~$p$, $a$ and~$x$:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $\mathrm{refInverse}(p, a) = a^{p - 2} \bmod p$, the inverse of~$a$ modulo~$p$ for a prime
      $p \nmid a$, by Fermat's little theorem;
    \item $\mathrm{refSquareRoot}(p, x)$ is the last even $y < p$ with $y^2 \equiv x \bmod p$,
      or~$p$ if there is none; for an odd prime~$p$ the even square root of a square is unique;
    \item $\mathrm{refIsSquare}(p, x)$, for an integer~$x$, says that the square root of $x \bmod p$
      is not~$p$: $x$ is a square modulo~$p$, $0$ included;
    \item $\mathrm{refHasInfinity}(\mathit{cs}, p)$ says that~$f_6$ is a square modulo~$p$, so that
      the curve has points at infinity modulo~$p$ (a quintic always has);
    \item $\mathrm{refIsFSquare}(\mathit{cs}, p, x)$ says that $F(x, 1) = f(x)$ is a square
      modulo~$p$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Horner's rule gives the remainder $s_1 X + s_2$ of~$f$ modulo $X^2 - b X + c$, as
$X^2 \equiv b X - c$. At the roots~$x_1$, $x_2$ of the quadratic,
$f(x_1) f(x_2) = (s_1 x_1 + s_2)(s_1 x_2 + s_2) = s_2^2 + b s_1 s_2 + c s_1^2$. If $s_1 X + s_2$ is
the remainder of $(v_1 X + v_0)^2$, then this norm is the square of
$N = c v_1^2 + b v_1 v_0 + v_0^2$, and $2 s_2 + b s_1 + 2 N = (b v_1 + 2 v_0)^2$.

\begin{definition}[The classes on the Jacobian modulo a prime]\label{def:refPointOnJ}
  \lean{JPoints.refRemainder, JPoints.refPointOnJ, JPoints.refIsPointOnJ}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:refIsSquare}
  Let $\mathit{cs}$ be a list of coefficients of degree~$\delta$, $p > 0$ and~$b$, $c$ natural
  numbers.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $\mathrm{refRemainder}(\mathit{cs}, \delta, p, b, c) = (s_1, s_2)$ is computed by Horner's
      rule as \texttt{point\_on\_j} computes it: from $(f_\delta \bmod p, f_{\delta - 1} \bmod p)$,
      for $n = \delta - 2, \ldots, 0$ the pair $(s_1 b + s_2,\ f_n + (p - c) s_1)$, each entry
      reduced modulo~$p$.
    \item $\mathrm{refPointOnJ}(\mathit{cs}, \delta, p, b, c)$ holds when
      $s_2^2 + b s_1 s_2 + c s_1^2$ is a square~$t^2$ modulo~$p$, with $t \ne p$ its square root
      recorded by $\mathrm{refSquareRoot}$, and one of $2 s_2 + b s_1 + 2 t$ and
      $2 s_2 + b s_1 - 2 t$ is a square modulo~$p$.
    \item $\mathrm{refIsPointOnJ}(\mathit{cs}, \delta, p, b, c)$, the entry of the exact table of
      the third stage, \texttt{is\_point\_on\_j}, holds on the diagonal $c \equiv b^2 / 4 \bmod p$,
      with~$1/4$ computed as $\lfloor (1 + p (3 - (p \mathbin{\&} 2))) / 4 \rfloor$, and off it when
      $\mathrm{refPointOnJ}(\mathit{cs}, \delta, p, b, c)$ holds.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The class tests of the stages]\label{def:refAdmits12}
  \lean{JPoints.refAdmits12, JPoints.refAdmits3}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:refIsSquare, def:refPointOnJ}
  For integers~$a$, $b$, $c$, let~$\bar a$, $\bar b$, $\bar c$ be their remainders modulo~$p$
  and~$\bar x^{-1}$ the inverse $\mathrm{refInverse}(p, \bar x)$.
  $\mathrm{refAdmits12}(\mathit{cs}, \delta, p, a, b, c)$, the class test of the first two stages,
  holds
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item for $\bar a = 0$, when $\bar b = 0$, or when $\mathrm{refHasInfinity}(\mathit{cs}, p)$ and
      $\mathrm{refIsFSquare}(\mathit{cs}, p, \bar c \bar b^{-1} \bmod p)$ hold;
    \item for $\bar a \ne 0$, when
      $\mathrm{refIsPointOnJ}(\mathit{cs}, \delta, p, \bar b \bar a^{-1} \bmod p, \bar c \bar a^{-1}
      \bmod p)$ holds.
  \end{enumerate}
  $\mathrm{refAdmits3}(\mathit{cs}, \delta, p, a, b, c)$, the class test of the third stage, is the
  same test without the condition $\mathrm{refHasInfinity}(\mathit{cs}, p)$.
\end{definition}

The rows of the sieve tables of the first two stages are built by \texttt{init\_sieve} from these
tests (Chapter~\ref{chap:words}); the third stage, in \texttt{row3\_setup} and \texttt{check\_bit},
does not read the table of the points at infinity, so that it admits every class that the first two
admit (\lname{JPoints.refAdmits3\_of\_refAdmits12}).

\formalnote{The C code reduces by multiplication with reciprocals (\texttt{MODP}, \texttt{MOD3});
the references take remainders, and the residues are natural numbers, as the indices of the tables
are. That the two agree is part of the contracts of the builders of the tables and of the stages
(Lemma~\ref{lem:pointOnJ_spec}, Chapter~\ref{chap:words}).}

The three lemmas that follow are read in~$\mathbb{F}_p$, where the identities of the Kummer surface
over~$\ZZ$ hold after the cast. They need neither the squarefreeness of~$f$ nor the coprimality of
the coordinates.

\begin{lemma}[Points prime to~$p$ reduce to classes on the Jacobian]\label{lem:refPointOnJ_of_lifts}
  \lean{JPoints.refPointOnJ_of_lifts}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:poly, def:kummer, def:Lifts, def:refPointOnJ}
  Let~$p$ be an odd prime and $\mathit{cs}$ a list of at most seven integers of degree
  $\delta \ge 5$. Let $(a, b, c, d)$ be integers with $K(a, b, c, d) = 0$ and
  $\mathrm{Lifts}(a, b, c, d)$, and $p \nmid a$. Then
  $\mathrm{refPointOnJ}(\mathit{cs}, \delta, p, b', c')$ holds for the values $b', c' < p$ of~$b/a$
  and~$c/a$ in~$\mathbb{F}_p$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:auxA2, def:refIsSquare}
  As $a \ne 0$, $A_2 = \alpha^2$ and $B_2 = \beta^2$ are squares, and on the Kummer surface the
  signs can be chosen with $2 \alpha \beta = C$, the polynomial of the identity
  $4 A_2 B_2 = C^2 - a^4 K$. With $v_1 = \alpha / a^2$ and $v_0 = \beta / a^2$ in~$\mathbb{F}_p$,
  the remainder of~$f$ modulo $X^2 - (b/a) X + c/a$ is that of $(v_1 X + v_0)^2$, by two identities
  in~$\mathbb{F}_p$. Its norm is then the square of~$N$, so that either square root $t = \pm N$ that
  the table records makes one of $2 s_2 + b s_1 \pm 2 t$ the square $(b v_1 + 2 v_0)^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Points with~$p$ dividing~$a$]\label{lem:isSquare_and_isSquare_sexticForm_of_lifts}
  \lean{JPoints.isSquare_and_isSquare_sexticForm_of_lifts}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:kummer, def:auxA2, def:Lifts}
  Let~$p$ be a prime, and let integers $(a, b, c, d)$ with $p \mid a$ and $p \nmid b$ satisfy
  $K(a, b, c, d) = 0$ and $\mathrm{Lifts}(a, b, c, d)$. Then~$f_6$ and $F(c, b)$ are squares
  modulo~$p$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  For $a = 0$ this is the lifting test. For $a \ne 0$, $A_2 \equiv f_6 b^4 \bmod p$ is a square, so
  that~$f_6$ is one. The polynomial~$A_2$ of the point $(c, b, a, d)$ of the reversed curve
  $y^2 = x^6 f(1/x)$ is a square~$\gamma^2$: $a^4$ times it is the square of
  $b c \alpha + (b^2 - a c) \beta$. Modulo~$p$ the Kummer equation gives
  $(f_5 c^2 - b d)^2 \equiv 4 f_6 \gamma^2$, and
  $F(c, b) \equiv b^2 \gamma^2 + b c^3 (f_5 c^2 - b d) + f_6 c^6$, whose product with~$b^4$ is the
  square of $b^3 \gamma' + \alpha c^3$ for a suitable sign $\gamma' = \pm \gamma$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Lifting points are admitted modulo every odd prime]\label{lem:refAdmits12_of_lifts}
  \lean{JPoints.refAdmits12_of_lifts}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:poly, def:kummer, def:Lifts, def:refAdmits12}
  Let~$p$ be an odd prime and $\mathit{cs}$ a list of at most seven integers of degree
  $\delta \ge 5$. For integers $(a, b, c, d)$ with $K(a, b, c, d) = 0$ and
  $\mathrm{Lifts}(a, b, c, d)$, $\mathrm{refAdmits12}(\mathit{cs}, \delta, p, a, b, c)$ holds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:auxA2, def:refIsSquare, def:refPointOnJ, lem:refPointOnJ_of_lifts, lem:isSquare_and_isSquare_sexticForm_of_lifts}
  For $p \nmid a$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:refPointOnJ_of_lifts}. For $p \mid a$ and $p \mid b$ the test
  admits the class. For $p \mid a$ and $p \nmid b$,
  Lemma~\ref{lem:isSquare_and_isSquare_sexticForm_of_lifts} gives the points at infinity and
  $F(c, b) = b^6 f(c / b)$ a square, hence~$f(c/b)$ a square modulo~$p$; the square roots of
  $\mathrm{refSquareRoot}$ exist exactly for the squares, as~$p$ is odd.
\end{proof}

The search sees triples, not points. A candidate over the triple $(a, b, c)$ is $(m a, m b, m c, n)$
for a root~$n/m$ of the Kummer equation in lowest terms (Definition~\ref{def:kummerRoots}), and on
it $K(m a, m b, m c, n) = m^2 (K_2 n^2 + m K_1 n + m^2 K_0)$. For $p \nmid m$ the class of the
candidate in the projective plane over~$\mathbb{F}_p$ is that of the triple, and the tests depend on
that class only (\lname{JPoints.refAdmits12\_mul}). For $p \mid m$, $p \nmid n$, and the equation
forces $K_2 = b^2 - 4 a c \equiv 0 \bmod p$: the triple lies on the conic, whose classes the test
always admits, those with $p \mid a$ because then $p \mid b$, the others on the diagonal of the
exact table.

\begin{lemma}[The triples of the points are admitted]\label{lem:refAdmits12_of_hasPointOver}
  \lean{JPoints.refAdmits12_of_hasPointOver}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:poly, def:HasPointOver, def:refAdmits12}
  Let $\mathit{cs}$ be a list of at most seven integers of degree $\delta \ge 5$ and~$p$ an odd
  prime. If a point lies over the triple $(a, b, c)$,
  $\mathrm{HasPointOver}(\mathit{cs}, (a, b, c))$, then
  $\mathrm{refAdmits12}(\mathit{cs}, \delta, p, a, b, c)$ holds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:kummer, def:Lifts, def:kummerRoots, lem:mem_kummerRoots_iff, def:refIsSquare, def:refPointOnJ, lem:refAdmits12_of_lifts}
  The point is a candidate $(m a, m b, m c, n)$ of a root~$n / m$
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:mem_kummerRoots_iff}) for which $\mathrm{Lifts}$ holds. For $p \nmid m$, by
  Lemma~\ref{lem:refAdmits12_of_lifts} for the candidate and the invariance of the test under
  scaling by a unit; for $p \mid m$, by the conic, as~$p$ does not divide the numerator~$n$.
\end{proof}

\section{The first coordinates of a monic quintic}\label{sec:sieveQuintic}

For a monic quintic the search takes only the first coordinates that are squares (the test of the
units of work, Definition~\ref{def:unitKeeps}); \texttt{normalise\_curve} makes a quintic with
leading coefficient~$-1$ monic (Definition~\ref{def:normalizeCurve}). The reason is the following
lemma, which holds for $f_5 = \pm 1$.

\begin{lemma}[For a monic quintic the first coordinate is a square]\label{lem:isSquare_of_mem_kummerPoints}
  \lean{JPoints.isSquare_of_mem_kummerPoints}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Lifts, def:kummerRoots}
  Let~$f_5$ be a unit and $f_6 = 0$. Let $a \ge 0$, $b$, $c$ be integers with
  $\gcd(a, \gcd(b, c)) = 1$, and let a candidate~$P$ of $\mathrm{kummerPoints}(f, (a, b, c))$
  satisfy $\mathrm{Lifts}$. Then~$a$ is a square.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:kummer, def:auxA2, lem:mem_kummerRoots_iff}
  For $a \ne 0$, let $P = (m a, m b, m c, n)$ and~$p$ a prime divisor of~$a$; it suffices that its
  exponent in~$a$ is even. As~$f_5$ is a unit, $p \nmid f_5$. For $p \nmid b$ the Kummer equation
  makes~$p$ prime to~$m$, and the square $A_2 = a m^3 Y$ has $Y \equiv f_5 m b^3 \bmod p$ prime
  to~$p$. For $p \mid b$, so that $p \nmid c$, the discriminant of the Kummer quadratic, a square,
  is~$16 a R$ with $R \equiv f_5^2 c^5 \bmod p$, where~$R$ is the resultant of~$f$ and
  $a x^2 - b x + c$. In both cases the exponent of~$p$ in~$a$ is even.
\end{proof}

\section{The condition at~2}\label{sec:sieveTwoAdic}

The condition at~$2$ tests the class of~$(a, b)$ modulo~$64$ of a row, and the word of the class
holds the admitted classes of~$c$ modulo~$64$. Let a point $(m a, m b, m c, n)$ that lifts be the
candidate of a root~$n/m$ over a triple with $a \ne 0$, and $A_0 = A_2(a, b, c, 0)$, so that
$A_2(a, b, c, d) = a^3 d + A_0$ and $A_2(m a, m b, m c, n) = m^3 (a^3 n + m A_0)$, a square. Write
$m = 2^e u$ with~$u$ odd and $t \equiv n / u \bmod 64$. The Kummer equation cleared of its
denominator is $K_2 t^2 + 2^e K_1 t + 4^e K_0 \equiv 0 \bmod 64$, with~$t$ odd for $e \ge 1$; for
$e = 0$ the square is $u^4 (a^3 t + A_0)$, for $e = 1$ it is $8 u^4 (a^3 t + 2 A_0)$, and for
$e \ge 6$ the equation modulo~$64$ is that of $e = 6$.

\begin{definition}[The condition at~2]\label{def:TwoadicCondition}
  \lean{JPoints.TwoadicCondition, JPoints.TwoadicAdmissible}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:kummer, def:auxA2}
  For~$k_2$, $k_1$, $k_0$, $a_3$, $A_0 \in \ZZ/64\ZZ$, $\mathrm{TwoadicCondition}$ holds when
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item some~$d$ has $k_2 d^2 + k_1 d + k_0 = 0$ and $a_3 d + A_0$ a square in $\ZZ/64\ZZ$; or
    \item some odd~$t$ has $k_2 t^2 + 2 k_1 t + 4 k_0 = 0$ and $8 (a_3 t + 2 A_0)$ a square; or
    \item for some $2 \le e \le 6$, some odd~$t$ has $k_2 t^2 + 2^e k_1 t + 4^e k_0 = 0$.
  \end{enumerate}
  $\mathrm{TwoadicAdmissible}(f, a, b, c)$ is this condition for the coefficients~$K_2$, $K_1$,
  $K_0$ of the Kummer quadratic over $(a, b, c)$, $a_3 = a^3$ and $A_0 = A_2(a, b, c, 0)$, all
  modulo~$64$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The points satisfy the condition at~2]\label{lem:twoadicAdmissible_of_hasPointOver}
  \lean{JPoints.twoadicAdmissible_of_hasPointOver}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:HasPointOver, def:TwoadicCondition}
  If $a \ne 0$ and $\mathrm{HasPointOver}(\mathit{cs}, (a, b, c))$, then
  $\mathrm{TwoadicAdmissible}(f, a, b, c)$ holds for the coefficients~$f$ of~$\mathit{cs}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:kummer, def:auxA2, def:Lifts, def:kummerRoots, lem:mem_kummerRoots_iff}
  The point is a candidate $(m a, m b, m c, n)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:mem_kummerRoots_iff}), and
  $\mathrm{Lifts}$ makes $A_2 = m^3 (a^3 n + m A_0)$ a square since $m a \ne 0$. With $m = 2^e u$
  and $t = n / u$ modulo~$64$, the cases $e = 0$, $e = 1$ and $e \ge 2$ give the three alternatives;
  for $e \ge 1$, $n$ and hence~$t$ are odd, as $\gcd(m, n) = 1$. Neither the degree nor the
  squarefreeness of the curve is needed, nor the coprimality of the triple.
\end{proof}

For each class~$(a, b)$ modulo~$64$, \texttt{twoadic\_table} builds
the word of the admitted~$c$ from the squares modulo~$64$, the solutions of the quadratic equations
modulo~$64$ and the coefficients of the curve and of the quartic~$K_0$ modulo~$64$, and flags the
classes with an admitted~$c$ as alive. \texttt{twoadic\_init} (\csource{j-points.c}{1741}{1753})
builds the table modulo~$64$ and uses the condition only when that table excludes some triple not
all even, with the density of the admitted triples and the share of the empty classes for the cost
model. A table modulo~$16$ could not decide this: it may admit every class where the table
modulo~$64$ does not.

\begin{definition}[The tables of the condition at~2]\label{def:IsTwoadicTable}
  \lean{JPoints.IsTwoadicTable}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:TwoadicCondition}
  $\mathrm{IsTwoadicTable}(\mathit{coeffs}, \mathit{masks}, \mathit{alive})$ says: for all
  $a_2, b_2, c_2 < 64$, not all even, at which $\mathrm{TwoadicAdmissible}$ holds for the
  coefficients of $\mathit{coeffs}$, the word $\mathit{masks}[64 a_2 + b_2]$ has the bit~$c_2$ and
  $\mathit{alive}[64 a_2 + b_2]$ is true.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The contract of \texttt{twoadic\_table}]\label{lem:twoadicTable_spec}
  \lean{JPoints.twoadicTable_spec}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:kummerInit, def:IsTwoadicTable}
  For the coefficients $k = \mathrm{kummerInit}(\mathit{coeffs})$ of the quartic~$K_0$ and arrays of
  $64 \cdot 64$ words and flags, \texttt{twoadic\_table} modulo~$64$ returns words and flags with
  $\mathrm{IsTwoadicTable}(\mathit{coeffs})$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:kummer, def:auxA2, def:TwoadicCondition}
  By the contracts of its parts: the squares, the solutions of the quadratic equations, the
  coefficients and the powers of the residues modulo~$64$ and the value of~$K_0$ are what the test
  of a triple reads; that test admits every triple at which the condition holds, and the row of a
  class, the classes and the table keep what it admits.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Sound conditions at~2]\label{def:TwoAdic.Sound}
  \lean{JPoints.TwoAdic.Sound}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:HasPointOver}
  $\mathrm{TwoAdic.Sound}(\mathit{tw}, \mathit{cs})$ says: when the sieve uses the condition at~$2$
  ($\mathit{use2}$), for every triple $(a, b, c)$ with $a \ne 0$ and $\gcd(a, \gcd(b, c)) = 1$ over
  which a point lies, $\mathrm{HasPointOver}(\mathit{cs}, (a, b, c))$, the class
  $(a \bmod 64, b \bmod 64)$ is alive ($\mathit{alive2}$) and its word of $\mathit{mask2}$ has the
  bit $c \bmod 64$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The condition at~2 is sound]\label{lem:TwoAdic.sound_of_isTwoadicTable}
  \lean{JPoints.TwoAdic.sound_of_isTwoadicTable, JPoints.TwoAdic.sound_of_use2_eq_false}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:IsTwoadicTable, def:TwoAdic.Sound}
  A condition at~$2$ whose words and flags satisfy $\mathrm{IsTwoadicTable}(\mathit{coeffs})$ is
  $\mathrm{TwoAdic.Sound}$ for the coefficients of $\mathit{coeffs}$, and every condition at~$2$
  that the sieve does not use is $\mathrm{TwoAdic.Sound}$ for every curve.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:kummer, def:auxA2, def:HasPointOver, def:TwoadicCondition, lem:twoadicAdmissible_of_hasPointOver}
  A coprime triple is not all even, and the condition at~$2$ holds at its residues modulo~$64$ by
  Lemma~\ref{lem:twoadicAdmissible_of_hasPointOver}, as it depends on these residues only. An unused
  condition has nothing to show.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The contract of \texttt{twoadic\_init}]\label{lem:twoadicInit_spec}
  \lean{JPoints.twoadicInit_spec}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:poly, def:kummerInit, def:TwoAdic.Sound}
  For seven coefficients $\mathit{coeffs}$ of degree at least~$5$ and
  $k = \mathrm{kummerInit}(\mathit{coeffs})$, \texttt{twoadic\_init} returns a condition at~$2$ with
  $\mathrm{TwoAdic.Sound}$ for the coefficients of $\mathit{coeffs}$: the table modulo~$64$, unused
  when it admits every triple not all even.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:IsTwoadicTable, lem:twoadicTable_spec, lem:TwoAdic.sound_of_isTwoadicTable}
  In either branch by Lemma~\ref{lem:TwoAdic.sound_of_isTwoadicTable}, with
  Lemma~\ref{lem:twoadicTable_spec} for the table modulo~$64$.
\end{proof}

\section{The tables of the program}\label{sec:sieveTables}

\texttt{init\_main} fills the tables that do not depend on the curve: the words with one bit set,
the inverses and the square roots modulo the primes of the table (\csource{j-points.c}{695}{702}).

\begin{definition}[The tables of \texttt{init\_main}]\label{def:Tables.WF}
  \lean{JPoints.Tables.WF}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:refIsSquare}
  $\mathrm{Tables.WF}(t)$ says: entry~$n$ of the words with one bit set is~$2^n$, for every bit
  position~$n$ of a word; and for every prime~$p$ of the table, the entry of the inverses of
  $0 < a < p$ is $\mathrm{refInverse}(p, a)$ and the entry of the square roots of $x < p$ is
  $\mathrm{refSquareRoot}(p, x)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The contract of \texttt{init\_main}]\label{lem:initMain_spec}
  \lean{JPoints.initMain_spec}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Tables.WF}
  From the initial tables, \texttt{init\_main} returns tables with $\mathrm{Tables.WF}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:refIsSquare}
  By the invariants of its loops: the words by the shifts of a bit; the inverses by \texttt{invert},
  $a^{p - 2}$ modulo~$p$ by binary exponentiation; the square roots by \texttt{init\_squares}, which
  records for each residue the last even root it meets.
\end{proof}

\texttt{point\_on\_j} reduces by \texttt{MODP}, a multiplication with the reciprocal
$\lceil 2^{32} / p \rceil$ and a shift. For $0 < p \le 256$ and the operands $0 \le x < 2^{24}$ that
occur, $\lfloor x \lceil 2^{32}/p \rceil / 2^{32} \rfloor = \lfloor x / p \rfloor$, since
$x (\lceil 2^{32}/p \rceil p - 2^{32}) < 2^{24} p \le 2^{32}$, so that \texttt{MODP} is the
remainder (\lname{JPoints.modp\_eq\_emod}).

\begin{lemma}[The contract of \texttt{point\_on\_j}]\label{lem:pointOnJ_spec}
  \lean{JPoints.pointOnJ_spec}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:refIsSquare, def:refPointOnJ}
  Let $\delta \le 6$ and $b, c < p$. For every curve $\mathit{cs}$ whose rows of the prime~$p$ the
  arguments of \texttt{point\_on\_j} hold (the square roots $\mathrm{refSquareRoot}(p, x)$, the
  coefficients of~$\mathit{cs}$ modulo~$p$, $0 < p \le 256$ and the reciprocal
  $\lceil 2^{32} / p \rceil$), \texttt{point\_on\_j} returns
  $\mathrm{refPointOnJ}(\mathit{cs}, \delta, p, b, c)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \texttt{MODP} is the remainder, so that the loop of Horner's rule computes
  $\mathrm{refRemainder}$, one coefficient at a time, and the tests of the norm and of
  $2 s_2 + b s_1 \pm 2 t$ read the square roots of $\mathrm{refSquareRoot}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The contract of \texttt{init\_table}]\label{lem:initTable_spec}
  \lean{JPoints.initTable_spec}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:refIsSquare, def:refPointOnJ, def:Tables.WF}
  Let $\mathrm{Tables.WF}(t)$, $\mathit{pn} < \mathrm{NUM\_PRIMES}$, $\delta \le 6$, and let the
  exact table of the third stage of the tables of the curve~$\mathit{ct}$ have its size. For every
  curve $\mathit{cs}$ whose coefficients modulo $p = \mathrm{prime}[\mathit{pn}]$ the
  tables~$\mathit{ct}$ hold, \texttt{init\_table} fills the rows of~$p$ of the exact table with
  $\mathrm{refIsPointOnJ}(\mathit{cs}, \delta, p, b, c)$ for $b, c < p$, and leaves the coefficients
  modulo the primes, the points at infinity, the values of~$f$ that are squares and the rows of the
  other primes as they were.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pointOnJ_spec}
  The diagonal $c \equiv b^2 / 4$ is tested first, as in $\mathrm{refIsPointOnJ}$, and the other
  entries by Lemma~\ref{lem:pointOnJ_spec}; the entries of one prime are told from those of another
  by the uniqueness of the positions $a M + b$ with $b < M$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[The tables of the curve]\label{def:CurveTables.WF}
  \lean{JPoints.CurveTables.WF, JPoints.CurveTables.admits12, JPoints.CurveTables.admits3}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:refIsSquare, def:refPointOnJ}
  $\mathrm{CurveTables.WF}(\mathit{ct}, \mathit{cs}, \delta, \mathit{nPrimes}, \mathit{pnn},
  \mathit{sp3})$ says: for each of the first $\mathit{nPrimes}$ primes~$p$ of the table (the primes
  considered), $\mathit{ct}$ holds the coefficients of~$\mathit{cs}$ modulo~$p$,
  $\mathrm{refHasInfinity}(\mathit{cs}, p)$ and $\mathrm{refIsFSquare}(\mathit{cs}, p, x)$ for
  $x < p$; and for each sieving prime $p = \mathrm{prime}[\mathit{pnn}[k]]$ with $k < \mathit{sp3}$,
  its rows of the exact table of the third stage are
  $\mathrm{refIsPointOnJ}(\mathit{cs}, \delta, p, b, c)$ for $b, c < p$.
  $\mathrm{CurveTables.admits12}$ and $\mathrm{CurveTables.admits3}$ are the class tests of
  Definition~\ref{def:refAdmits12} read from the tables~$\mathit{ct}$ as \texttt{init\_sieve},
  \texttt{row3\_setup} and \texttt{check\_bit} read them.
\end{definition}

For tables with $\mathrm{CurveTables.WF}$, the tests read from the tables are the references at
every sieving prime (\lname{JPoints.CurveTables.WF.admits12\_eq},
\lname{JPoints.CurveTables.WF.admits3\_eq}).

\section{The choice of the sieve}\label{sec:sieveChoice}

\texttt{init\_fmodpsquare} makes the plan of the sieve: it computes the tables of the curve and the
rate of each prime considered, the share of the residue triples modulo the prime that the sieve
admits, and then \texttt{choose\_primes} chooses the sieving primes and the mode by a cost model.

\begin{definition}[The plan of the sieve]\label{def:Plan}
  \lean{JPoints.Plan, JPoints.IsStages}
  \leanok
  A $\mathrm{Plan}$ holds the indices $\mathit{pnn}$ in \texttt{prime[]} of the sieving primes,
  those of the first stage, then of the second, then of the third; the numbers
  $\mathit{sieve\_primes1}$, $\mathit{sieve\_primes2}$ and $\mathit{sieve\_primes3}$ of the primes
  of the first stage, of the first two and of all three; the mode, $0$ for the box, $1$ for the tube
  and~$2$ for the real region; and whether the words of the tube are cut to the real region
  (\csource{j-points.c}{229}{230}, \csource{j-points.c}{270}{272}).
  $\mathrm{IsStages}(\mathit{nPrimes}, \mathit{pin3}, P, \phi, \mathit{sp1}, \mathit{sp2},
  \mathit{sp3})$ says that $\mathit{sp1} \le \mathit{sp2} \le \mathit{sp3}$, that
  $\mathit{sp3} \le \mathit{pin3}$ when $\mathit{pin3} \ge 0$ (the value of \texttt{-N} when it is
  given), that the indices $\phi(j)$, $j < \mathit{sp3}$, are distinct and below $\mathit{nPrimes}$,
  and that $P(\phi(j))$ holds for $j < \mathit{sp2}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The choice of the sieve]\label{def:initFmodpsquare}
  \lean{JPoints.initFmodpsquareRates, JPoints.choosePrimes, JPoints.initFmodpsquare}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Plan, def:Cli.Input, def:Double.add, def:Double.ofDyadic}
  $\mathrm{initFmodpsquare}$ is \texttt{init\_fmodpsquare}. Its first part,
  $\mathrm{initFmodpsquareRates}$, fills the tables of the curve at the first \texttt{num\_primes}
  primes and their records in \texttt{prec}, each with its rate (\csource{j-points.c}{808}{812}), and
  sorts the records by rate with \texttt{qsort} (\csource{j-points.c}{857}{857}). $\mathrm{choosePrimes}$, \texttt{choose\_primes}
  (\csource{j-points.c}{907}{926}), prices the modes in this order: the box, the region and, for a
  bounded fourth coordinate, the tube without and with the cut; it takes the first and each one
  cheaper than the cheapest before, with its numbers of primes and its primes. Then the rows of the
  exact table of the third stage of the primes chosen are filled, and unless \texttt{-q} is given,
  messages~$4$, $7$ and~$8$ are printed (\csource{j-points.c}{865}{871}).
\end{definition}

\subsection{The rates of the primes}

For a prime~$p$ considered, $\mathit{np}_0 = 1 + \#\{x < p : f(x) \text{ a square modulo } p\}$
counts the classes $(0 : 0 : 1)$ and $(0 : 1 : x)$ with~$f(x)$ a square, and $\mathit{np}_1$ the
admitted classes $(1 : b : c)$, all of them by \texttt{init\_table} for
$p^2 \le \mathrm{SAMPLE\_PAIRS}$, otherwise estimated from a sample. Since each class of the
projective plane over~$\mathbb{F}_p$ has~$p - 1$ representatives and the zero triple is admitted,
\texttt{rate\_of} (\csource{j-points.c}{751}{753}) gives the rate
$((p - 1)(\mathit{np}_0 + \mathit{np}_1) + 1) / p^3$, computed in doubles in the order of the C
code; the records are sorted by \texttt{qsort} with \texttt{compare\_entries}, the sign of the
difference of the rates (\csource{j-points.c}{742}{746}). The proofs need of the sort only that it
permutes, whatever the comparison.

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{qsort} permutes]\label{lem:toList_qsort_perm}
  \lean{JPoints.toList_qsort_perm}
  \leanok
  For every comparison $\mathit{cmp}$ and array $\mathit{base}$ of size~$n$, the list of
  $\mathrm{qsort}(\mathit{cmp}, \mathit{base}, n)$ is a permutation of the list of $\mathit{base}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Each recursive call of the merge sort \texttt{msort\_with\_tmp} merges the two halves that it
  sorts, which permutes, also when a NaN makes the comparison inconsistent.
\end{proof}

\formalnote{\texttt{qsort} is a function of the C library: the model is the merge sort that glibc
uses when it can allocate its buffer, and leaves out its fallback to a heap sort.}

\begin{definition}[The rates of the primes]\label{def:Rates.WF}
  \lean{JPoints.Rates.WF}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:refIsSquare, def:refPointOnJ}
  $\mathrm{Rates.WF}(r, \mathit{ct}, \mathit{cs}, \delta, \mathit{nPrimes})$ says:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item at each of the first $\mathit{nPrimes}$ primes~$p$ of the table, the tables of the
      curve~$\mathit{ct}$ hold the coefficients of~$\mathit{cs}$ modulo~$p$,
      $\mathrm{refHasInfinity}$ and $\mathrm{refIsFSquare}$;
    \item the rows of the exact table of the third stage of each prime marked in $\mathit{r.tabled}$
      are those of $\mathrm{refIsPointOnJ}$;
    \item $\mathit{r.tabled}$ and the exact table have their sizes;
    \item the first $\mathit{nPrimes}$ records of $\mathit{r.prec}$ hold the indices of the first
      $\mathit{nPrimes}$ primes in some order, each with its prime
      ($\mathrm{PrecWF}$).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The contract of the first part of \texttt{init\_fmodpsquare}]\label{lem:initFmodpsquareRates_spec}
  \lean{JPoints.initFmodpsquareRates_spec}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:poly, def:Tables.WF, def:Rates.WF, def:initFmodpsquare}
  From tables with $\mathrm{Tables.WF}$, $\mathit{nPrimes} \le \mathrm{NUM\_PRIMES}$,
  coefficients $\mathit{coeffs}$ of degree at most $\delta \le 6$, and tables of the curve and
  records of their sizes, $\mathrm{initFmodpsquareRates}$ returns tables of the curve and
  records with $\mathrm{Rates.WF}$ for the coefficients of $\mathit{coeffs}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:auxA2, def:refIsSquare, def:refPointOnJ, lem:initTable_spec}
  For each prime, the coefficients modulo~$p$, the values of~$f$ by Horner's rule, which of them
  and~$f_6$ are squares, and for the primes whose rows are filled, Lemma~\ref{lem:initTable_spec};
  the tables of the other primes stay as they were; the sort permutes the records.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The choice of the primes and of the mode}

For each mode, \texttt{choose\_primes\_mode} ranks the primes up to $\mathrm{MAX\_TABLE\_PRIME}$ for
the first stage by their value for it, the reduction $-\log r$ of the survivors per cycle of a pass
and of its tables; for each number~$n_1$ of primes of the first stage it ranks the others for the
second stage, and for each number~$n_2$ of primes of the first two stages all primes left for the
third; it prices every admissible $(n_1, n_2, n_3)$ and keeps the cheapest, which the options
\texttt{-n}, \texttt{-M} and \texttt{-N} may pin. Whatever the costs, a choice that it keeps takes
the first~$n_1$ candidates of the first ranking, the first $n_2 - n_1$ of the second, which are the
candidates of the first that the first stage does not take, and the first $n_3 - n_2$ of the third,
the primes that neither takes; each ranking holds its candidates once, sorted by~\texttt{qsort}. So
the three parts are disjoint, and the records of \texttt{prec} make their indices those of distinct
primes of the table.

\begin{lemma}[The contract of \texttt{choose\_primes}]\label{lem:choosePrimes_spec}
  \lean{JPoints.choosePrimes_spec}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Plan, def:initFmodpsquare, def:Cli.Input}
  Let \texttt{prec} hold records of the first $\mathit{nPrimes} \le \mathrm{NUM\_PRIMES}$ primes
  with $\mathrm{PrecWF}$, $\mathit{nPrimes}$ the value of \texttt{num\_primes}, and let the plan
  have $\mathrm{NUM\_PRIMES} + 1$ entries $\mathit{pnn}$. Then, for every value of the
  floating-point numbers of the cost model, \texttt{choose\_primes} returns a plan whose stages
  satisfy $\mathrm{IsStages}$ for $\mathit{nPrimes}$, the value of \texttt{-N}, the indices
  $\mathit{pnn}$ and the property $\mathrm{prime}[n] \le \mathrm{MAX\_TABLE\_PRIME}$, and which,
  when it has sieving primes, has the mode~$0$, $1$ or~$2$, the tube only for a bounded fourth
  coordinate.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:toList_qsort_perm, def:Double.ofDyadic}
  The structural argument above, with Lemma~\ref{lem:toList_qsort_perm} for the rankings; the ranges
  of the numbers keep them within the rankings and the pin of \texttt{-N}. Each contract holds for
  every outcome of each comparison of costs.
\end{proof}

\formalnote{No proof reads a floating-point number of the cost model, which runs in Lean's
\texttt{Float} with the \texttt{log} and \texttt{pow} of the platform's C library. So the mode is
known only when there are sieving primes: \texttt{choose\_primes} takes a mode when
$\mathit{best} < 0$ or its cost is below $\mathit{best}$, from $\mathit{best} = -1$, and a proof
that does not evaluate these comparisons cannot exclude that it takes none, in which case the plan
has no sieving primes. Without sieving primes, \texttt{init\_fmodpsquare} sets the box and drops the
condition at~$2$, before message~$4$ reports the mode.}

\begin{definition}[A choice of the sieve]\label{def:IsChoice}
  \lean{JPoints.IsChoice, JPoints.choiceText}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:CurveTables.WF, def:Plan, def:initFmodpsquare}
  $\mathrm{IsChoice}(g, \mathit{ct}, \mathit{plan})$ says:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item the sieving primes of the plan are distinct primes among those considered, the numbers of
      the stages do not decrease and are at most \texttt{num\_primes}, the primes of the first two stages
      are at most $\mathrm{MAX\_TABLE\_PRIME}$, and there are no sieving primes when \texttt{-N} is~$0$;
    \item $\mathrm{CurveTables.WF}$ holds for the primes considered and the sieving primes;
    \item with sieving primes, the mode is~$0$, $1$ or~$2$, the tube only for a bounded fourth
      coordinate, and without them the mode is~$0$, the box.
  \end{enumerate}
  $\mathrm{choiceText}(t, g, \mathit{plan})$ is the text that the choice prints, messages~$4$, $7$
  and~$8$, empty under \texttt{-q}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The contract of \texttt{init\_fmodpsquare}]\label{lem:initFmodpsquare_isChoice}
  \lean{JPoints.initFmodpsquare_isChoice, JPoints.initFmodpsquare_spec, JPoints.choiceText_of_quiet}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:poly, def:Cli.Input.WF, def:Tables.WF, def:Plan, def:initFmodpsquare, def:IsChoice}
  Let~$t$ be tables with $\mathrm{Tables.WF}$, $g$ globals of the choice whose command line has
  $\mathrm{Cli.Input.WF}$ and whose curve has seven coefficients of degree at least~$5$, and let the

  of the cost model, \texttt{init\_fmodpsquare}, from the initial tables of the curve and records,
  returns tables of the curve~$\mathit{ct}$, records $\mathit{prec}$ and a plan $\mathit{plan}'$
  with $\mathrm{IsChoice}(g, \mathit{ct}, \mathit{plan}')$, the condition at~$2$
  of~$g$, not used when $\mathit{plan}'$ has no sieving primes, and prints
  $\mathrm{choiceText}(t, g, \mathit{plan})$, which is empty under \texttt{-q}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Cli.Input, def:refIsSquare, def:refPointOnJ, def:CurveTables.WF, lem:initTable_spec, def:Rates.WF, lem:initFmodpsquareRates_spec, lem:choosePrimes_spec}
  The rates by Lemma~\ref{lem:initFmodpsquareRates_spec}, the plan by
  Lemma~\ref{lem:choosePrimes_spec}, and the rows of the primes chosen by
  Lemma~\ref{lem:initTable_spec}, so that $\mathrm{CurveTables.WF}$ holds for the sieving primes;
  without sieving primes the box and the condition at~$2$ unused by the step that follows the

\end{proof}

\section{Why the choice does not change the output}\label{sec:sieveOutput}

The search reads the choice only through the set-up that \texttt{find\_points} makes from it,
$\mathrm{IsSieveSetup}$ (Definition~\ref{def:IsSearchSetup}), which holds for every value of the
floating-point numbers of the cost model: among others the stages, the tables of the curve and the
mode of~$\mathrm{IsChoice}$, and the condition at~$2$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:twoadicInit_spec}. Under
this set-up alone, the class tests and the condition at~$2$ keep every triple over which a point
lies.

\begin{lemma}[The tables of the curve admit the points]\label{lem:CurveTables.admits12_of_hasPointOver}
  \lean{JPoints.CurveTables.admits12_of_hasPointOver, JPoints.CurveTables.admits3_of_hasPointOver}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:HasPointOver, def:CheckGlobals, def:CurveTables.WF, def:IsSearchSetup}
  Let $\mathrm{IsSieveSetup}(\mathit{bg}, \mathit{sd})$ hold, and let a point lie over $(a, b, c)$
  on the curve searched, that of the globals of the exact check. Then for every
  $n < \mathit{sieve\_primes3}$ the class tests $\mathrm{CurveTables.admits12}$ and
  $\mathrm{CurveTables.admits3}$ of the tables of the curve admit $(a, b, c)$ at the~$n$-th sieving
  prime.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:poly, def:Cli.Input, def:Cli.Input.WF, def:CheckGlobals.WF, def:IsSearchGlobals, def:refIsSquare, def:refPointOnJ, def:refAdmits12, lem:refAdmits12_of_hasPointOver}
  By $\mathrm{CurveTables.WF}$ the tests are the references at the sieving prime, an odd prime of
  the table among the primes considered; the curve searched has seven coefficients of degree at
  least~$5$ ($\mathrm{CheckGlobals.WF}$). Then Lemma~\ref{lem:refAdmits12_of_hasPointOver}, and for
  the third stage the inclusion of the classes of the first two.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The condition at~2 keeps the rows of the points]\label{lem:aliveKeeps_of_hasPointOver}
  \lean{JPoints.aliveKeeps_of_hasPointOver}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:HasPointOver, def:rowKeeps, def:CheckGlobals, def:IsSearchSetup}
  Let $\mathrm{IsSieveSetup}(\mathit{bg}, \mathit{sd})$ hold, let $\gcd(a, \gcd(b, c)) = 1$, and let
  a point lie over $(a, b, c)$ on the curve searched. Then the test
  $\mathrm{aliveKeeps}(\mathit{bg.sift}, a, b)$ holds: the condition at~$2$ does not drop the
  row~$(a, b)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:IsSearchGlobals, def:TwoAdic.Sound}
  The set-up holds a condition at~$2$ with $\mathrm{TwoAdic.Sound}$; the test passes when the
  condition is unused or $a = 0$, and otherwise by its field $\mathit{alive2}$.
\end{proof}

From these lemmas, the tests of the rows, of the third coordinates and of the units of work
(Lemmas~\ref{lem:rowKeeps_of_hasPointOver}, \ref{lem:colKeeps_of_hasPointOver}
and~\ref{lem:unitKeeps_of_hasPointOver}, the last by Lemma~\ref{lem:isSquare_of_mem_kummerPoints})
and the test of the bands in each of the three modes (Lemma~\ref{lem:bandKeeps_of_points_ne_nil})
keep every triple over which a point lies, under $\mathrm{IsSieveSetup}$ alone. The exact check then
prints exactly the points among the survivors (Lemma~\ref{lem:checkOnePoint_spec}), and the search
lists them in the order of its triples, which the tests only filter
(Lemma~\ref{lem:textOf_firstIf_flatMap_orderedTriples}). So the primes and the mode chosen decide
how many triples reach the exact check, not which points the program prints nor their order. Without
\texttt{-q} the program also prints the text of the choice, $\mathrm{choiceText}$, and the numbers
of the survivors, which depend on the choice; the theorem on such a run states the points between
these texts (Theorem~\ref{thm:Program.exists_run_eq_of_readInput_eq_ok_of_quiet_eq_false}).

As no proof can tell which mode the cost model takes when there are sieving primes, the main
theorems need what every mode needs. The real region and the tube rely on the isolating intervals of
the real roots that \texttt{real\_roots\_init} computes, by Sturm's theorem
(Chapter~\ref{chap:doubles}); hence the hypothesis on Sturm's theorem in the main theorems
(Theorem~\ref{thm:Program.run_eq_of_readInput_eq_ok_of_quiet}), unless the sieve has no primes at all, which \texttt{-N 0} gives, and then the search runs over
the box.
