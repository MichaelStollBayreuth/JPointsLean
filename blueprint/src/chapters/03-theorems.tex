\chapter{The main theorems}\label{chap:theorems}

This chapter states what is proved about the program of the model, \lname{JPoints.Program.run}
(Definition~\ref{def:Program.run}), the counterpart of \jpoints{}: on a command line that
\texttt{read\_input} accepts, under \texttt{-q}, it prints the points of the specification in the
order in which \jpoints{} finds them, each in the format of \texttt{-f}, and exits with the
status~$0$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:Program.run_eq_of_readInput_eq_ok_of_quiet}); without \texttt{-q} it
prints the same text inside the messages of its report
(Theorem~\ref{thm:Program.exists_run_eq_of_readInput_eq_ok_of_quiet_eq_false}). The points of the
specification are the points of the curve given, each once (Section~\ref{sec:theoremsPoints}), so
that under the default format the points that the program prints, read back from its text, are
exactly the points of the curve, each once (Section~\ref{sec:theoremsReadBack}).
Section~\ref{sec:theoremsProofs} outlines the proofs from the contracts that
Chapters~\ref{chap:model} to~\ref{chap:region} prove: the search takes the triples of the
specification in their order and drops only triples over which no point lies, and the exact check
prints exactly the points over each triple that it is given. Comparator certifies six of the
statements of this chapter, together with the four of Chapter~\ref{chap:spec} on the brute-force
reference (Section~\ref{sec:comparator}).

Throughout, $\mathit{args}$ is a command line (the words after the name of the program) and~$I$ the
globals of the command line (Definition~\ref{def:Cli.Input}) that \texttt{read\_input} leaves when it
accepts it (Definition~\ref{def:Cli.readInput}). The statements speak about the points of the
specification for the coefficients, the height bound, \texttt{-a} and \texttt{-w} of the command
line,
\[ P_I = \mathrm{orderedPoints}(\mathit{cs}_I, H_I, \mathit{all}_I, W_I) \]
(Definition~\ref{def:orderedPoints}), where $\mathit{cs}_I$ is the list of the seven coefficients
$I.\mathrm{coeffs}$, $H_I$ the height bound $I.\mathrm{height}$ as a natural number,
$\mathit{all}_I = I.\mathrm{allPoints}$ the flag of~\texttt{-a}, and~$W_I$ the bound
$I.\mathrm{dbound}$ on the fourth coordinate when $I.\mathrm{dbounded}$ holds (the value of
\texttt{-w}, or the height bound) and none otherwise (under \texttt{-a} without \texttt{-w};
Definition~\ref{def:Cli.Input.dboundOption}). The theorems on the program take some of the following
hypotheses.
\begin{itemize}
  \item~$h_i$: $\mathrm{readInput}(\mathit{args}) = \mathrm{ok}(I)$, \texttt{read\_input} accepts
    the command line.
  \item~$h_q$: $I.\mathrm{quiet} = \mathrm{true}$, the option \texttt{-q} is given.
  \item~$h_f$: $\mathrm{parseFormat}(\texttt{l}, I.\mathrm{printFormat}) = \mathrm{some}(f)$, the
    model admits the format of \texttt{-f}, with the pieces~$f$
    (Definition~\ref{def:Printf.parseFormat}).
  \item~$h_S$: $I.\mathrm{sievePrimes3} = 0 \lor \mathrm{SturmTheorem}$, Sturm's theorem
    (Definition~\ref{def:SturmTheorem}) unless the sieve has no primes at all, which
    \texttt{-N} with a value of at most~$0$ gives.
\end{itemize}

\formalnote{Sturm's theorem, which Mathlib lacks, is a hypothesis, a proposition stated in
Mathlib's terms, not an axiom. It is needed for the isolating intervals of the real roots that
\texttt{real\_roots\_init} computes, on which the real region and the tube rely
(Chapter~\ref{chap:doubles}). With \texttt{-N} at most~$0$ no stage of the sieve has primes, the
search runs in the box, which reads none of these intervals, and the theorems hold outright.}

\formalnote{No hypothesis bounds the height: \texttt{read\_input} refuses height bounds above
\texttt{MAX\_SEARCH\_HEIGHT}, which is~$2^{26}$ (\lname{JPoints.maxSearchHeight}). Up to it the
products of \texttt{row\_region} stay below~$2^{53}$, where the rounding of the ends of its excluded
intervals stays within their margins (Chapter~\ref{chap:region}); at larger heights the real region
could drop words that hold points.}

\formalnote{The model refuses a format of \texttt{-f} that it does not admit, with the
status~$11$, before any message, where \jpoints{} prints the points with it
(Definition~\ref{def:Program.run}). The admitted formats are those, of at most four conversions,
that \texttt{printf} and \texttt{gmp\_printf} print as the model does; the hypothesis~$h_f$
restricts the theorems to them.}

\section{The points of the specification}\label{sec:theoremsPoints}

The program searches a transformed curve, reversed or negated (Definition~\ref{def:searchedCoeffs}),
and takes its points back to the curve given (Definition~\ref{def:backPoint}). The points of the
specification are nevertheless exactly the points of the curve given.

\begin{theorem}[The points of the specification]\label{thm:mem_orderedPoints_iff}
  \lean{JPoints.mem_orderedPoints_iff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:poly, def:IsPoint, def:orderedPoints}
  Let $\mathit{cs}$ be a list of at most seven integers with $\mathrm{degree}(\mathit{cs}) \ge 5$
  and $\mathrm{poly}(\mathit{cs})$ squarefree (Definition~\ref{def:poly}), a curve that the program
  does not refuse. Then for all~$H$, $\mathit{all}$, $W$ and~$p$,
  \[ p \in \mathrm{orderedPoints}(\mathit{cs}, H, \mathit{all}, W) \iff
     \mathrm{IsPoint}(\mathit{cs}, H, \mathit{all}, W, p)\,. \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:kummer, def:Lifts, def:IsNormalized, def:InBounds, def:searchedCoeffs, def:backPoint, lem:squarefree_poly_searchedCoeffs, lem:isPoint_backPoint_iff, def:bruteForce, thm:mem_bruteForce_iff, thm:orderedPoints_eq_map_bruteForce}
  The points of the specification are those of the brute-force reference of the curve searched,
  taken back (Theorem~\ref{thm:orderedPoints_eq_map_bruteForce}). The curve searched has at most
  seven coefficients and degree at least~$5$, and it is squarefree
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:squarefree_poly_searchedCoeffs}), so that its brute force holds exactly its points
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:mem_bruteForce_iff}). The reversal of a sextic with $f_0 = 0$ and the negation
  of a quintic keep the Kummer equation, the lifting, the normalization and the bounds, so a point
  of the curve searched taken back is a point of the curve given and conversely
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:isPoint_backPoint_iff}); the map back is its own inverse on the normalized
  points.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The points of the specification are distinct]\label{thm:nodup_orderedPoints}
  \lean{JPoints.nodup_orderedPoints}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:orderedPoints}
  For all $\mathit{cs}$, $H$, $\mathit{all}$ and~$W$, the list
  $\mathrm{orderedPoints}(\mathit{cs}, H, \mathit{all}, W)$ has no duplicates.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:kummer, def:Lifts, def:IsNormalized, def:InBounds, def:IsPoint, def:orderedTriples, def:kummerRoots, def:searchedCoeffs, def:backPoint, lem:isPoint_backPoint_iff, def:bruteForce, thm:nodup_bruteForce, thm:bruteForce_eq_filter_flatMap_kummerPoints, thm:orderedPoints_eq_map_bruteForce}
  The brute force of the curve searched has no duplicates (Theorem~\ref{thm:nodup_bruteForce}),
  its elements are normalized, and the map back to the curve given is injective on the normalized
  points (Theorem~\ref{thm:orderedPoints_eq_map_bruteForce}).
\end{proof}

\section{What the program prints}\label{sec:theoremsProgram}

Under \texttt{-1} the program stops at the first point that it finds. The text of a point is that of
its coordinates under the format of \texttt{-f} by the rules of \texttt{printf}, whatever their
size. The exact check prints it by \texttt{printf} on machine words when the coordinates are small
enough for its path on machine words, and by \texttt{gmp\_printf} otherwise, under the format that
\texttt{printf\_mpz} builds for it, whose flags, widths and precisions \texttt{gmp\_printf} reads as
\texttt{printf} reads those of the format of \texttt{-f} (Lemma~\ref{lem:Printf.printMpz_gmpSpec}).

\begin{definition}[The first point only under \texttt{-1}]\label{def:firstIf}
  \lean{JPoints.firstIf}
  \leanok
  For a flag~$b$ and a list~$L$, $\mathrm{firstIf}(b, L)$ is~$L$ when~$b$ is false, and the list of
  the first element of~$L$ (empty if~$L$ is) when~$b$ is true: what the program prints of the points
  that it finds under \texttt{-1}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The text of a point]\label{def:pointText}
  \lean{JPoints.pointText}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Printf.parseFormat}
  Let~$f$ be the pieces of an admitted format and $p = (a, b, c, d)$ a point of the curve given.
  The text $\mathrm{pointText}(f, p)$ is what \texttt{printf} prints under~$f$ for the four
  coordinates, whatever their size: each conversion prints its coordinate by the rules of
  \texttt{printf} for a \texttt{long}, \lname{JPoints.Printf.printLong}. We write
  $\mathrm{pointText}(f)$ for the map $p \mapsto \mathrm{pointText}(f, p)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The bound of the command line]\label{def:Cli.Input.dboundOption}
  \lean{JPoints.Cli.Input.dboundOption}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Cli.Input}
  For the globals~$I$ of the command line, $I.\mathrm{dboundOption}$ is the bound~$W_I$ on the
  fourth coordinate.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The main theorem]\label{thm:Program.run_eq_of_readInput_eq_ok_of_quiet}
  \lean{JPoints.Program.run_eq_of_readInput_eq_ok_of_quiet}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:orderedPoints, def:SturmTheorem, def:firstIf, def:pointText, def:Cli.Input.dboundOption, def:Cli.Input, def:Cli.Failure, def:Cli.readInput, def:Printf.parseFormat, def:Program.run}
  Assume~$h_i$, $h_q$, $h_f$ and~$h_S$. Then the program prints the points of the
  specification in their order, only the first under \texttt{-1}, each in the format of
  \texttt{-f}, and exits with the status~$0$:
  \[ \mathrm{run}(\mathit{args}) = \Bigl(\mathrm{join}\bigl(\mathrm{map}(\mathrm{pointText}(f),\
     \mathrm{firstIf}(I.\mathrm{onePoint}, P_I))\bigr),\ 0\Bigr)\,. \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:orderedTriples, def:HasPointOver, lem:textOf_firstIf_flatMap_orderedTriples, lem:Program.mainSearch_spec, def:Cli.Input.WF, lem:Cli.readInput_spec, def:CheckGlobals, def:kummerInit, def:Printf.emitPoint, def:normalizeCurve, def:message, def:Tables.WF, lem:initMain_spec}
  On a command line that \texttt{read\_input} accepts with an admitted format, the program prints
  the head of the report (\lname{JPoints.Program.mainHead}), empty under \texttt{-q}, and the text
  of the search of \texttt{main} for the tables of \texttt{init\_main}, and exits with the
  status~$0$. The tables are well formed (Lemma~\ref{lem:initMain_spec}) and so are the globals of
  the command line (Lemma~\ref{lem:Cli.readInput_spec}), so that the contract of the search of
  \texttt{main} (Lemma~\ref{lem:Program.mainSearch_spec}) gives the text that the exact check prints
  for the points over the triples of the curve searched, only the first under \texttt{-1}; it is the
  text of the points of the specification
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:textOf_firstIf_flatMap_orderedTriples}).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The run without \texttt{-q}]\label{thm:Program.exists_run_eq_of_readInput_eq_ok_of_quiet_eq_false}
  \lean{JPoints.Program.exists_run_eq_of_readInput_eq_ok_of_quiet_eq_false}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:orderedPoints, def:SturmTheorem, def:firstIf, def:pointText, def:Cli.Input.dboundOption, def:Cli.Input, def:Cli.Failure, def:Cli.readInput, def:CheckGlobals, def:kummerInit, def:Printf.parseFormat, def:normalizeCurve, def:message, def:Program.run, def:Plan, def:IsChoice, def:Double.add, def:Double.ofDyadic, def:realRootsInit, def:rowRegion}
  Assume~$h_i$, $h_f$ and~$h_S$, and that \texttt{-q} is not given,
  $I.\mathrm{quiet} = \mathrm{false}$. Then there are totals~$T$ of the counters for which the
  program exits with the status~$0$ and prints, in this order:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item the head of the report (\lname{JPoints.Program.mainHead});
    \item the message of \texttt{normalise\_curve} (Definition~\ref{def:normalizeCurve});
    \item the text of the choice of the sieve (\lname{JPoints.choiceText}) for the tables of
      \texttt{init\_main}, the globals of the choice that \texttt{main} sets
      (\lname{JPoints.choiceGlobalsOf}) and the initial plan (Definition~\ref{def:Plan});
    \item the text of the points of
      Theorem~\ref{thm:Program.run_eq_of_readInput_eq_ok_of_quiet};
    \item messages~$12$ and~$2$ (Definition~\ref{def:message}) for the totals~$T$, the numbers of
      the survivors of the stages and of the points found.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:orderedTriples, def:HasPointOver, lem:textOf_firstIf_flatMap_orderedTriples, lem:Program.mainSearch_spec, def:Cli.Input.WF, lem:Cli.readInput_spec, def:Printf.emitPoint, def:Tables.WF, lem:initMain_spec}
  As for Theorem~\ref{thm:Program.run_eq_of_readInput_eq_ok_of_quiet}, by the contract of the
  search of \texttt{main} without \texttt{-q} (Lemma~\ref{lem:Program.mainSearch_spec}(2)) and the
  text of the points (Lemma~\ref{lem:textOf_firstIf_flatMap_orderedTriples}).
\end{proof}

\formalnote{The counters of the report are not verified: the theorem says only that the report
closes with messages~$12$ and~$2$ for some totals.}

\section{The points read back}\label{sec:theoremsReadBack}

Under the default format \verb|(%ld, %ld, %ld, %ld)\n| a point is printed as its four coordinates
in decimal on a line of its own, by \texttt{printf} and by \texttt{gmp\_printf} alike. Such a text
can be read back: each point ends at its newline, and the decimal numbers in it are separated by
a comma and a space. So the text that the program prints determines the list of the
points.

\begin{definition}[The text of a point under the default format]\label{def:defaultPointText}
  \lean{JPoints.defaultPointText}
  \leanok
  The text of the point $(a, b, c, d)$ under the default format,
  $\mathrm{defaultPointText}(a, b, c, d)$, is
  \texttt{(}$a$\texttt{, }$b$\texttt{, }$c$\texttt{, }$d$\texttt{)} followed by a newline, the four
  coordinates in decimal: what \texttt{printf} prints for the default format
  \verb|(%ld, %ld, %ld, %ld)\n|.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The points read back]\label{thm:Program.eq_firstIf_orderedPoints_of_run_eq}
  \lean{JPoints.Program.eq_firstIf_orderedPoints_of_run_eq}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:orderedPoints, def:SturmTheorem, def:firstIf, def:defaultPointText, def:Cli.Input.dboundOption, def:Cli.Input, def:Cli.Failure, def:Cli.readInput, def:Program.run}
  Assume~$h_i$, $h_q$ and~$h_S$, and that the format of \texttt{-f} is the default,
  $I.\mathrm{printFormat} = $ \verb|"(%ld, %ld, %ld, %ld)\n"|. If the text that the program prints is
  that of a list~$L$ of points,
  \[ \mathrm{run}(\mathit{args})_1 = \mathrm{join}(\mathrm{map}(\mathrm{defaultPointText}, L))\,, \]
  then $L = \mathrm{firstIf}(I.\mathrm{onePoint}, P_I)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pointText, thm:Program.run_eq_of_readInput_eq_ok_of_quiet, def:Printf.parseFormat}
  The model admits the default format, under which the text of a point
  (Definition~\ref{def:pointText}) is $\mathrm{defaultPointText}$. So
  Theorem~\ref{thm:Program.run_eq_of_readInput_eq_ok_of_quiet} gives
  \[ \mathrm{join}(\mathrm{map}(\mathrm{defaultPointText}, L)) =
     \mathrm{join}(\mathrm{map}(\mathrm{defaultPointText},
     \mathrm{firstIf}(I.\mathrm{onePoint}, P_I)))\,, \]
  and the concatenation of these texts is injective: the first text ends at the first newline, and
  its decimal numbers, separated by a comma and a space, determine the coordinates.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The printed points are the points]\label{thm:Program.mem_iff_isPoint_of_run_eq}
  \lean{JPoints.Program.mem_iff_isPoint_of_run_eq}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:IsPoint, def:SturmTheorem, def:defaultPointText, def:Cli.Input.dboundOption, def:Cli.Input, def:Cli.Failure, def:Cli.readInput, def:Program.run}
  Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:Program.eq_firstIf_orderedPoints_of_run_eq}, assume
  moreover that \texttt{-1} is not given, $I.\mathrm{onePoint} = \mathrm{false}$. If
  \[ \mathrm{run}(\mathit{args})_1 = \mathrm{join}(\mathrm{map}(\mathrm{defaultPointText}, L))\,, \]
  then for every~$p$,
  \[ p \in L \iff \mathrm{IsPoint}(\mathit{cs}_I, H_I, \mathit{all}_I, W_I, p)\,. \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:poly, def:orderedPoints, thm:mem_orderedPoints_iff, def:firstIf, thm:Program.eq_firstIf_orderedPoints_of_run_eq, def:Cli.Input.WF, lem:Cli.readInput_spec}
  By Theorem~\ref{thm:Program.eq_firstIf_orderedPoints_of_run_eq}, $L = P_I$, as
  $I.\mathrm{onePoint} = \mathrm{false}$. A command line that
  \texttt{read\_input} accepts has seven coefficients of a squarefree polynomial of degree at
  least~$5$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:Cli.readInput_spec}), so Theorem~\ref{thm:mem_orderedPoints_iff}
  applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Each point is printed once]\label{thm:Program.nodup_of_run_eq}
  \lean{JPoints.Program.nodup_of_run_eq}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:SturmTheorem, def:defaultPointText, def:Cli.Input, def:Cli.Failure, def:Cli.readInput, def:Program.run}
  Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:Program.eq_firstIf_orderedPoints_of_run_eq}, if
  \[ \mathrm{run}(\mathit{args})_1 = \mathrm{join}(\mathrm{map}(\mathrm{defaultPointText}, L))\,, \]
  then~$L$ has no duplicates.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:orderedPoints, thm:nodup_orderedPoints, def:firstIf, thm:Program.eq_firstIf_orderedPoints_of_run_eq}
  By Theorem~\ref{thm:Program.eq_firstIf_orderedPoints_of_run_eq}, $L$ is a sublist of~$P_I$, which
  has no duplicates (Theorem~\ref{thm:nodup_orderedPoints}).
\end{proof}

\section{The proofs in outline}\label{sec:theoremsProofs}

The proofs go from the program down to the contracts of its components. The search of
\texttt{main} prints, by the exact check, the points over the triples of the specification of the
curve searched, in their order (Section~\ref{sec:theoremsSearch}); the text that the exact check
prints for them is the text of the points of the specification (Section~\ref{sec:theoremsText}).
The search itself takes the triples in the order of the specification: the triple $(0, 0, 1)$, the
triples of the double points and then the main search, unit of work by unit of work, row by row,
the third coordinate increasing. The main search is a filtered enumeration: it drops units of work,
rows and third coordinates by Boolean tests computed from what the program reads
(Section~\ref{sec:theoremsFilters}). Each test is sound, keeping every triple over which a point
lies, and for such a test $\tau$ and every list~$T$ of triples
\[ \mathrm{flatMap}(\mathrm{points}, \mathrm{filter}(\tau, T)) = \mathrm{flatMap}(\mathrm{points},
   T)\,, \]
so that the points over the triples that the search keeps are the points over all of them. The
exact check, given a triple, computes the roots of the Kummer equation as the specification does
and checks each candidate (Lemma~\ref{lem:checkOnePoint_spec}): it prints exactly the points over
the triple, so that no point is printed that the specification does not have, and every point of
the specification over a triple that the search keeps is printed.

\subsection{The points over the triples}\label{sec:theoremsReference}

\begin{definition}[The points over a triple]\label{def:HasPointOver}
  \lean{JPoints.HasPointOver, JPoints.pointsOver}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Lifts, def:IsPoint, def:kummerRoots}
  For a coefficient list~$\mathit{cs}$ and a triple~$t$, a point \emph{lies over}~$t$,
  $\mathrm{HasPointOver}(\mathit{cs}, t)$, when some candidate $(a, b, c, d)$ of
  $\mathrm{kummerPoints}$ over~$t$ (Definition~\ref{def:kummerRoots}) lifts to the Jacobian
  (Definition~\ref{def:Lifts}), whatever the bounds of the search. The points over~$t$,
  $\mathrm{pointsOver}(\mathit{cs}, H, \mathit{all}, W, t)$, are the candidates over~$t$ that are
  points, $\mathrm{IsPoint}(\mathit{cs}, H, \mathit{all}, W)$ (Definition~\ref{def:IsPoint}), in the
  order of the roots of the Kummer equation.
\end{definition}

For the globals~$\mathit{gl}$ of the exact check (Definition~\ref{def:CheckGlobals}), which name
the curve searched and the bounds of the search, $\mathit{gl}.\mathrm{points}(t)$ is
$\mathrm{pointsOver}$ for these (\lname{JPoints.CheckGlobals.points}).

\begin{lemma}[The points of the specification over the triples]\label{lem:orderedPoints_eq_map_flatMap_pointsOver}
  \lean{JPoints.orderedPoints_eq_map_flatMap_pointsOver}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:orderedTriples, def:searchedCoeffs, def:backPoint, def:orderedPoints, def:HasPointOver}
  For all $\mathit{cs}$, $H$, $\mathit{all}$ and~$W$, with $(\mathit{cs}', \mathit{rev},
  \mathit{neg}) = \mathrm{searchedCoeffs}(\mathit{cs})$,
  \[ \mathrm{orderedPoints}(\mathit{cs}, H, \mathit{all}, W) =
     \mathrm{map}(\mathrm{backPoint}(\mathit{rev}, \mathit{neg}), Q)\,, \]
  where~$Q$ is the list of the points over the triples of the search of the curve searched,
  \[ Q = \mathrm{flatMap}(\mathrm{pointsOver}(\mathit{cs}', H, \mathit{all}, W),
     \mathrm{orderedTriples}(H))\,. \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:IsPoint, def:kummerRoots}
  The filter by $\mathrm{IsPoint}$ commutes with the concatenation over the triples.
\end{proof}

On the conic $b^2 = 4ac$ the leading coefficient $K_2 = b^2 - 4ac$ of the Kummer equation vanishes;
the coprime triples on it are $(0, 0, 1)$ and the triples $(s^2, 2st, t^2)$ of the double points,
which \jpoints{} searches apart from the others.

\begin{definition}[The parts of the search]\label{def:mainTriples}
  \lean{JPoints.conicTriples, JPoints.doubleTriples, JPoints.mainTriples, JPoints.unitTriples}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:orderedTriples}
  $\mathrm{conicTriples}(H)$ and $\mathrm{mainTriples}(H)$ are the triples of
  $\mathrm{orderedTriples}(H)$ on and off the conic $b^2 = 4ac$, in lexicographic order.
  $\mathrm{doubleTriples}(H)$ lists the triples $(s^2, 2st, t^2)$ of the double points in the order
  of \texttt{find\_double\_points}: the $s \ge 1$ with $s^2 \le H$, increasing, and for each~$s$ the
  $t$ coprime to~$s$ with $t^2 \le H$ and $|2st| \le H$, increasing. $\mathrm{unitTriples}(H, a)$
  lists the triples of $\mathrm{mainTriples}(H)$ with the first coordinate~$a$, the second
  coordinate increasing and for each the third (for $a = 0$ the rows $b \ge 1$).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The search in its parts]\label{lem:flatMap_orderedTriples}
  \lean{JPoints.orderedTriples_eq_append, JPoints.conicTriples_eq, JPoints.flatMap_orderedTriples}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:orderedTriples, def:mainTriples}
  For every~$H$:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $\mathrm{orderedTriples}(H) = \mathrm{conicTriples}(H) \mathbin{+\!\!+}
      \mathrm{mainTriples}(H)$;
    \item for $H \ge 1$, $\mathrm{conicTriples}(H)$ is $(0, 0, 1)$ followed by
      $\mathrm{doubleTriples}(H)$, and for $H = 0$ it is $\mathrm{doubleTriples}(H)$;
    \item for $H \ge 1$ and every function~$g$ from triples to lists,
      $\mathrm{flatMap}(g, \mathrm{orderedTriples}(H))$ is $g(0, 0, 1)$ followed by
      $\mathrm{flatMap}(g, \mathrm{doubleTriples}(H))$ and $\mathrm{flatMap}(g,
      \mathrm{mainTriples}(H))$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:mem_orderedTriples_iff}
  (1) holds by definition, and (3) follows from (1) and~(2). For~(2): a coprime triple with
  $b^2 = 4ac$ and $a > 0$ is $(s^2, 2st, t^2)$ with $s > 0$ and $\gcd(s, t) = 1$, and the only one
  with $a = 0$ is $(0, 0, 1)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:mem_orderedTriples_iff}); both lists are in
  lexicographic order and have the same elements, so they are equal.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The text of the points}\label{sec:theoremsText}

The exact check prints a point that it finds by \texttt{printf} when its coordinates are at most
the bounds $\mathit{gl}.\mathrm{hmax}$ and $\mathit{gl}.\mathrm{dmax}$ of its path on machine words,
and by \texttt{gmp\_printf} otherwise, after taking it back to the curve given
(\lname{JPoints.CheckGlobals.text}).

\begin{lemma}[The text of the exact check]\label{lem:CheckGlobals.text_eq_pointText}
  \lean{JPoints.CheckGlobals.text_eq_pointText}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:IsNormalized, def:backPoint, def:pointText, def:CheckGlobals, def:Printf.parseFormat, def:Printf.emitPoint}
  Let $\mathit{gl}$ be globals of the exact check whose format parses to the pieces~$f$,
  $\mathrm{parseFormat}(\texttt{l}, \mathit{gl}.\mathrm{printFormat}) = \mathrm{some}(f)$, and let
  $p$ be a normalized point (Definition~\ref{def:IsNormalized}). Then the text that the exact check
  prints for the point~$p$ of the curve searched is
  \[ \mathit{gl}.\mathrm{text}(p)
       = \mathrm{pointText}(f, \mathrm{backPoint}(\mathit{rev}, \mathit{neg}, p))\,, \]
  for the flags $\mathit{rev}$ and~$\mathit{neg}$ of the reversal and of the negation of the curve
  searched.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:Printf.emitPoint_eq_render, lem:Printf.printMpz_gmpSpec}
  Both paths print the point taken back: \texttt{printf} under the pieces~$f$, and
  \texttt{gmp\_printf} under the pieces of~$f$ rewritten by \lname{JPoints.Printf.Piece.gmpSpec}
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:Printf.emitPoint_eq_render}), which give the same text
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:Printf.printMpz_gmpSpec}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The text of the search]\label{lem:textOf_firstIf_flatMap_orderedTriples}
  \lean{JPoints.textOf_firstIf_flatMap_orderedTriples}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:orderedTriples, def:orderedPoints, def:firstIf, def:pointText, def:Cli.Input.dboundOption, def:HasPointOver, def:Cli.Input, def:CheckGlobals, def:kummerInit, def:Printf.parseFormat, def:Printf.emitPoint, def:normalizeCurve}
  Assume~$h_f$, and let $\mathit{gl}$ be the globals of the exact check that \texttt{main} sets
  for~$I$ (\lname{JPoints.checkGlobalsOf}). Then the text that the exact check prints for the
  points $\mathrm{firstIf}(I.\mathrm{onePoint}, \mathrm{flatMap}(\mathit{gl}.\mathrm{points},
  \mathrm{orderedTriples}(H_I)))$ of the curve searched is
  \[ \mathrm{join}\bigl(\mathrm{map}(\mathrm{pointText}(f),\
     \mathrm{firstIf}(I.\mathrm{onePoint}, P_I))\bigr)\,. \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:kummer, def:Lifts, def:IsNormalized, def:InBounds, def:IsPoint, def:kummerRoots, def:searchedCoeffs, def:backPoint, lem:orderedPoints_eq_map_flatMap_pointsOver, lem:CheckGlobals.text_eq_pointText}
  By Lemma~\ref{lem:orderedPoints_eq_map_flatMap_pointsOver}, $P_I$ is the list of the points over
  the triples of the curve searched, taken back, and $\mathrm{firstIf}$ commutes with the map back.
  Each point of the list lies over a triple and is a point, so it is normalized, and its text is
  that of the point taken back (Lemma~\ref{lem:CheckGlobals.text_eq_pointText}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The contract of the search of \texttt{main}]\label{lem:Program.mainSearch_spec}

\subsection{The filtered enumeration}\label{sec:theoremsFilters}

The main search takes the units of work, the first coordinates~$a$, and for each the rows $(a, b)$
of \texttt{run\_a} (\csource{j-points.c}{1330}{1343}), from a first row~$b_0$ by the step~$m$ of
\texttt{row\_step} (\csource{j-points.c}{1275}{1286}), up to~$H$. In the box each row is sieved by
\texttt{sift} (Lemma~\ref{lem:sift_spec}), in the tube and the real region the rows by
\texttt{sift\_bands} (Lemma~\ref{lem:siftBands_spec}); the sieve of a row hands the third
coordinates that it keeps to the exact check. The tests below say which units, rows and third
coordinates the search keeps, in the terms of the globals~$g$ of the sieve and the static data
$\mathit{sd}$ that the program reads.

\begin{definition}[The test of the units of work]\label{def:unitKeeps}
  \lean{JPoints.unitKeeps}
  \leanok
  $\mathrm{unitKeeps}(\mathit{curve}, a)$ is true when~$a$ is a square or the curve searched is not
  a monic quintic (of degree~$5$ with $f_5 = 1$): for a monic quintic the units of work of the main
  search are the squares $a = k^2$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The test of the rows]\label{def:rowKeeps}
  \lean{JPoints.aliveKeeps, JPoints.rowKeeps}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:CheckGlobals}
  $\mathrm{aliveKeeps}(g, a, b)$ holds when $a = 0$, or the sieve does not use the condition at~$2$,
  or the class of $(a \bmod 64, b \bmod 64)$ is alive under it. $\mathrm{rowKeeps}(g, a, b)$ holds
  when the step~$m$ of the rows of~$a$ divides~$b$ and $\mathrm{aliveKeeps}(g, a, b)$ holds; the
  step, the value of \texttt{row\_step}, is the product of the sieving primes $p \mid a$ modulo
  which the curve has no points at infinity, or $H + 1$ as soon as this product exceeds~$H$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The tests of the three stages]\label{def:stage1Keeps}
  \lean{JPoints.stage1Keeps, JPoints.stage2Keeps, JPoints.stage3Keeps}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:CurveTables.WF}
  $\mathrm{stage1Keeps}(g, a, b, c)$ holds when every sieving prime of the first stage admits the
  class of $(a, b, c)$ by the class test $\mathrm{admits12}$ of the tables of the curve
  (Definition~\ref{def:CurveTables.WF}); $\mathrm{stage2Keeps}(g, a, b, c)$ likewise for the primes
  of the second stage, and $\mathrm{stage3Keeps}(g, a, b, c)$ for those of the third stage by the
  class test $\mathrm{admits3}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The test of the third coordinates]\label{def:colKeeps}
  \lean{JPoints.maskKeeps, JPoints.rangeKeeps, JPoints.coprimeKeeps, JPoints.colKeeps}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:stage1Keeps, def:CheckGlobals}
  $\mathrm{maskKeeps}(g, a, b, c)$ holds when the mask of the row $(a, b)$ has the bit
  $c \bmod 64$: the mask is the word of the condition at~$2$ when the sieve uses it and $a \ne 0$,
  otherwise that of the odd~$c$ when~$a$ and~$b$ are even, and that of all~$c$ otherwise.
  $\mathrm{rangeKeeps}(g, \mathit{sd}, c)$ holds when~$c$ lies in the row: its word
  $\lfloor c/64 \rfloor$ among the words of a row, and its bit in the masks of the first and the
  last word, which is $|c| \le H$ (\lname{JPoints.rangeKeeps\_eq\_decide\_abs\_le}).
  $\mathrm{coprimeKeeps}(a, b, c)$ holds when $\gcd(a, b, c) = 1$, as \texttt{relprime3} tests.
  Finally, $\mathrm{colKeeps}(g, \mathit{sd}, a, b, c)$ is the conjunction of the tests of the three
  stages, $\mathrm{maskKeeps}$, $\mathrm{rangeKeeps}$ and $\mathrm{coprimeKeeps}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The test of the bands]\label{def:bandKeeps}
  \lean{JPoints.bandRanges, JPoints.bandKeeps}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:tubeCell, def:bandOf, def:CheckGlobals, def:Double.ofDyadic, def:rowRegion}
  $\mathrm{bandRanges}(g, \mathit{sd}, a, b_{\mathrm{first}}, m, b)$ are the ranges of words of the
  row $(a, b)$ that \texttt{sift\_bands} sieves, for the rows from $b_{\mathrm{first}}$ on by the
  step~$m$: from the initial state of the analysis, the analysis of the tube for~$a$ and of the band
  of~$b$ (Definition~\ref{def:bandOf}), then the ranges of the row: in the tube those of the band,
  cut to the real region of the row when \texttt{tube\_cut} is set, and in the real region those of
  \texttt{row\_region}.
  $\mathrm{bandKeeps}(g, \mathit{sd}, a, b_{\mathrm{first}}, m, b, c)$ holds for every~$c$ in the
  box, and in the tube and the real region when the word $\lfloor c/64 \rfloor$ lies in one of
  these ranges.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The points of the filtered enumeration]\label{def:rowPoints}
  \lean{JPoints.rowsFrom, JPoints.rowPoints, JPoints.bandRowPoints, JPoints.bandsPoints}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:orderedTriples, def:HasPointOver, def:rowKeeps, def:colKeeps, def:bandKeeps, def:CheckGlobals}
  $\mathrm{rowsFrom}(b_0, m, H)$ lists $b_0, b_0 + m, b_0 + 2m, \dots$ up to~$H$ (none when
  $b_0 > H$). $\mathrm{rowPoints}(g, \mathit{sd}, a, b)$ lists the points over the triples
  $(a, b, c)$ with $-H \le c \le H$, $c$ increasing, off the conic, $b^2 \ne 4ac$, for which
  $\mathrm{colKeeps}(g, \mathit{sd}, a, b, c)$ holds. $\mathrm{bandRowPoints}$ does the same with
  $\mathrm{bandKeeps}$ added, and $\mathrm{bandsPoints}(g, \mathit{sd}, a, b_{\mathrm{first}}, m)$
  lists the points of $\mathrm{bandRowPoints}$ over the rows~$b$ of
  $\mathrm{rowsFrom}(b_{\mathrm{first}}, m, H)$ that $\mathrm{aliveKeeps}$ leaves, in order.
\end{definition}

The contracts of the sieve of a row and of the sieve by bands say that they print these lists
(Lemmas~\ref{lem:sift_spec} and~\ref{lem:siftBands_spec}). Each test keeps every triple over which
a point lies. The tests of the units, the rows and the third coordinates keep the triples over
which a point of the Kummer surface that lifts lies, whatever the bounds on the point; the test of
the bands keeps the triples off the conic over which a point within the bounds lies
(Lemma~\ref{lem:bandKeeps_of_points_ne_nil}), since the tube uses the bound on the fourth
coordinate.

\begin{lemma}[Soundness of the test of the units of work]\label{lem:unitKeeps_of_hasPointOver}
  \lean{JPoints.unitKeeps_of_hasPointOver}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:HasPointOver, def:unitKeeps, def:CheckGlobals, def:CheckGlobals.WF}
  Let $\mathit{gl}$ be globals of the exact check as \texttt{main} sets them
  (Definition~\ref{def:CheckGlobals.WF}), $a \ge 0$, and $(a, b, c)$ a triple with
  $\gcd(a, b, c) = 1$ over which a point of the curve searched lies. Then
  $\mathrm{unitKeeps}(\mathit{gl}.\mathrm{curve}, a)$ holds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:poly, def:Lifts, def:kummerRoots, lem:isSquare_of_mem_kummerPoints}
  For a monic quintic the first coordinate of a coprime triple over which a point lies is a square
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:isSquare_of_mem_kummerPoints}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Soundness of the test of the rows]\label{lem:rowKeeps_of_hasPointOver}
  \lean{JPoints.rowKeeps_of_hasPointOver}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:HasPointOver, def:rowKeeps, def:CheckGlobals, def:IsSearchSetup}
  Let the globals of the sieve by bands and the static data be as \texttt{find\_points} sets them up
  (\lname{JPoints.IsSieveSetup}, Definition~\ref{def:IsSearchSetup}), and $(a, b, c)$ a triple with
  $|b| \le H$ and $\gcd(a, b, c) = 1$ over which a point of the curve searched lies. Then
  $\mathrm{rowKeeps}(g, a, b)$ holds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Cli.Input, def:Cli.Input.WF, def:CheckGlobals.WF, def:IsSearchGlobals, def:refIsSquare, def:CurveTables.WF, lem:CurveTables.admits12_of_hasPointOver, lem:aliveKeeps_of_hasPointOver, def:rowStepProd, lem:run_rowStep}
  Let $p \mid a$ be a sieving prime modulo which the curve has no points at infinity. The class test
  of the first two stages admits the class of $(a, b, c)$
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:CurveTables.admits12_of_hasPointOver}), which forces $p \mid b$. So the product
  of these primes divides~$b$, and the step of \texttt{row\_step} is this product
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:run_rowStep}), or it exceeds~$H$, and then $b = 0$. The condition at~$2$ keeps
  the class of $(a, b)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:aliveKeeps_of_hasPointOver}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Soundness of the test of the third coordinates]\label{lem:colKeeps_of_hasPointOver}
  \lean{JPoints.colKeeps_of_hasPointOver}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:orderedTriples, def:HasPointOver, def:colKeeps, def:CheckGlobals, def:IsSearchSetup}
  Under the set-up of Lemma~\ref{lem:rowKeeps_of_hasPointOver}, let $(a, b, c)$ be a triple of
  $\mathrm{boxTriples}(H)$ (Definition~\ref{def:orderedTriples}) over which a point of the curve
  searched lies. Then $\mathrm{colKeeps}(g, \mathit{sd}, a, b, c)$ holds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:mem_orderedTriples_iff, def:stage1Keeps, def:Cli.Input, def:Cli.Input.WF, def:CheckGlobals.WF, def:IsSearchGlobals, def:Tables.WF, def:CurveTables.WF, lem:CurveTables.admits12_of_hasPointOver, def:TwoAdic.Sound, def:SieveTables.WF, def:SiftData.WF, def:IsStageSetup}
  Each part of the test keeps the triple. The class tests of the three stages admit the class of
  a triple over which a point lies at every sieving prime
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:CurveTables.admits12_of_hasPointOver}). The mask: the condition at~$2$ admits
  every point (Definition~\ref{def:TwoAdic.Sound}), and when~$a$ and~$b$ are even, $c$~is odd, as
  the triple is coprime. The row: $|c| \le H$ for a triple of the box, whose coordinates are also
  coprime (Lemma~\ref{lem:mem_orderedTriples_iff}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The rows of a first coordinate hold its points]\label{lem:flatMap_rowPoints_rowsFrom}
  \lean{JPoints.flatMap_rowPoints_rowsFrom, JPoints.bandsPoints_rowStart}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:HasPointOver, def:mainTriples, def:rowKeeps, def:rowPoints, def:CheckGlobals, def:IsSearchSetup}

  $b_0 = m$ for $a = 0$ and $b_0 = -\lfloor H/m \rfloor\, m$ otherwise, the first row of
  \texttt{run\_a}. Then the points of $\mathrm{rowPoints}(g, \mathit{sd}, a, b)$ over the rows~$b$
  of $\mathrm{rowsFrom}(b_0, m, H)$ that $\mathrm{aliveKeeps}$ leaves, in order, and likewise
  $\mathrm{bandsPoints}(g, \mathit{sd}, a, b_0, m)$, are the points
  $\mathrm{flatMap}(\mathit{gl}.\mathrm{points}, \mathrm{unitTriples}(H, a))$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:kummer, def:Lifts, def:IsNormalized, def:InBounds, def:IsPoint, def:orderedTriples, def:kummerRoots, lem:mem_orderedTriples_iff, def:stage1Keeps, def:colKeeps, def:bandKeeps, lem:rowKeeps_of_hasPointOver, lem:colKeeps_of_hasPointOver, def:Cli.Input, def:Cli.Input.WF, def:CheckGlobals.WF, def:IsSearchGlobals, def:rowStepProd, lem:run_rowStep, lem:bandKeeps_of_points_ne_nil}
  The rows from~$b_0$ by the step~$m$ up to~$H$ are the rows of the box whose second coordinate
  $m$~divides, with $b > 0$ for $a = 0$. The rows that the step or the condition at~$2$ drops
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:rowKeeps_of_hasPointOver}), the third coordinates that the sieve of a row drops
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:colKeeps_of_hasPointOver}) and, in the bands, those that the analysis of the band
  drops (Lemma~\ref{lem:bandKeeps_of_points_ne_nil}) have no points over them.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The units of work hold the points of the main search]\label{lem:flatMap_range_unitTriples_unitA}
  \lean{JPoints.flatMap_range_unitTriples_unitA}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:HasPointOver, def:mainTriples, def:CheckGlobals, def:CheckGlobals.WF}
  Let $\mathit{gl}$ be globals of the exact check as \texttt{main} sets them, and let the number of
  the units of work be $\lfloor\sqrt{H}\rfloor + 1$ for a monic quintic and $H + 1$ otherwise, the
  first coordinate of the unit~$k$ being $k^2$ for a monic quintic and~$k$ otherwise. Then the
  points $\mathrm{flatMap}(\mathit{gl}.\mathrm{points}, \mathrm{unitTriples}(H, a_k))$ over the
  first coordinates~$a_k$ of the units, in turn, are the points $\mathrm{flatMap}(\mathit{gl}.\mathrm{points},
  \mathrm{mainTriples}(H))$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:kummer, def:Lifts, def:IsNormalized, def:InBounds, def:IsPoint, def:orderedTriples, def:kummerRoots, def:unitKeeps, lem:unitKeeps_of_hasPointOver}
  The main triples are those of the first coordinates $0 \le a \le H$ in turn. For a monic quintic
  the first coordinates that are not squares have no points over their triples
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:unitKeeps_of_hasPointOver}), and the squares up to~$H$ are the $k^2$ with
  $k \le \lfloor\sqrt{H}\rfloor$.
\end{proof}

\section{What comparator certifies}\label{sec:comparator}

Comparator certifies ten statements, those that \texttt{comparator/challenges.json} lists. The
challenge restates the specification verbatim, together with the definitions on top of it that the
theorems on the program use: Sturm's theorem (Definition~\ref{def:SturmTheorem}), the first point
only under~\texttt{-1} (Definition~\ref{def:firstIf}), the text of a point under an admitted format
(Definition~\ref{def:pointText}) and under the default format
(Definition~\ref{def:defaultPointText}). It imports the model, whose definitions it does not
restate: the brute-force reference, the program \lname{JPoints.Program.run}, the command line that
it reads and the formats of~\texttt{-f}. The ten statements are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the four on the brute-force reference: Theorems~\ref{thm:mem_bruteForce_iff},
    \ref{thm:nodup_bruteForce}, \ref{thm:bruteForce_eq_filter_flatMap_kummerPoints}
    and~\ref{thm:orderedPoints_eq_map_bruteForce};
  \item the points of the specification and their distinctness:
    Theorems~\ref{thm:mem_orderedPoints_iff} and~\ref{thm:nodup_orderedPoints};
  \item the output of the program under \texttt{-q}:
    Theorem~\ref{thm:Program.run_eq_of_readInput_eq_ok_of_quiet};
  \item the points read back, the printed points are the points, and each is printed once:
    Theorems~\ref{thm:Program.eq_firstIf_orderedPoints_of_run_eq},
