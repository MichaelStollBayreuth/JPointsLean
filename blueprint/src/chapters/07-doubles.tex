\chapter{Doubles and the real roots}\label{chap:doubles}

\jpoints{} computes with C \texttt{double}s where they decide what is searched: in the analysis of
the tube (Chapter~\ref{chap:tube}), in the excluded ranges of the real region
(Chapter~\ref{chap:region}), at the ends of the intervals on which~$f$ is negative, in two
reciprocals of the index arithmetic of the sieve and in the counts of some loops
(Chapter~\ref{chap:words}). The model holds a double as its IEEE~754 binary64 bits, with a function
on the bits for each operation, as gcc, glibc and GMP compute it on x86-64
(Chapter~\ref{chap:model}). The first half of this chapter is what the proofs know of these
functions: the values of the doubles and their order, the roundings and their errors, and a
calculus of the errors of a computation, counted in roundings, in which the analysis of the tube is
carried out. The second half is \texttt{real\_roots\_init}: the Sturm chain of~$f$
and~$f'$ in exact rational arithmetic, the isolation of the real roots of~$f$ by the counts of the
chain, and the record of the gaps between the roots on which~$f$ is negative, with ends that are
doubles. Mathlib does not have Sturm's theorem; it is a hypothesis
(Definition~\ref{def:SturmTheorem}), under which these gaps are proved to be where~$f$ is negative,
as the real region needs it; the main theorems assume it unless the sieve has no primes at all (Chapter~\ref{chap:theorems}).

\section{The values of the doubles}\label{sec:doubleValues}

A double is a structure of its $64$~bits, the sign bit, an exponent field~$e$ of $11$~bits and a
fraction~$t$ of $52$, with no equality of its own: equality of the bits is not \texttt{==}, under
which $-0.0$ equals~$0.0$ and a NaN equals nothing. A double is finite when $e < 2047$, an infinity
when $e = 2047$ and $t = 0$, and a NaN otherwise. Two values are attached to the bits.

\begin{definition}[The value of a finite double]\label{def:Double.toRat}
  \lean{JPoints.Double.toRat}
  \leanok
  For a finite double~$x$, $\bar x = \mathrm{toRat}(x) \in \QQ$ is its exact value $m\,2^k$: for a
  normal~$x$ ($e \ge 1$) the significand $m = 2^{52} + t$ and $k = e - 1075$, for a subnormal~$x$
  or a zero ($e = 0$) $m = t$ and $k = -1074$, and~$m$ negated when the sign bit is set; both zeros
  have the value~$0$, as \texttt{mpq\_set\_d} of GMP sets it. It is not meant for infinities and
  NaNs.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The value of a double]\label{def:doubleValue}
  \lean{JPoints.doubleValue}
  \leanok
  The value $v(x) \in \QQ \cup \{\bot, \top\}$ (Mathlib's \lname{WithBotTop}~$\QQ$) of the bits of a
  double~$x$, with the sign of the top bit: $\pm t\,2^{-1074}$ for $e = 0$ (both zeros giving~$0$),
  $\pm(2^{52} + t)\,2^{e - 1075}$ for $1 \le e \le 2046$, and $\bot$ and~$\top$ for the
  infinities~$-\infty$ and~$+\infty$. The value of a NaN is junk, an infinity.
\end{definition}

On finite doubles the two agree (\lname{JPoints.Double.doubleValue\_eq\_toRat}). The comparisons
\texttt{<}, \texttt{<=} and \texttt{==} of C are false when either side is a NaN, and otherwise
compare the \emph{order values}: the bits without the sign bit, negated when the sign bit is set
(\lname{JPoints.Double.orderValue}). The order value is~$0$ for both zeros and runs from $-\Omega$
to~$\Omega$, $\Omega = 2047 \cdot 2^{52}$ the order value of~$+\infty$
(\lname{JPoints.Double.maxOrderValue}); write $\nu(k)$ for the value of the doubles of the order
value~$k$
(\lname{JPoints.Double.valueOfOrderValue}). The neighbors $\mathrm{nextUp}(x)$
and~$\mathrm{nextDown}(x)$ of a double~$x$ are \texttt{nextafter(x, HUGE\_VAL)} and
\texttt{nextafter(x, -HUGE\_VAL)} of \texttt{math.h}, where \texttt{HUGE\_VAL} is~$+\infty$.

\begin{lemma}[The order of the doubles]\label{lem:Double.lt_eq_true_iff}
  \lean{JPoints.Double.lt_eq_true_iff, JPoints.Double.strictMonoOn_valueOfOrderValue, JPoints.Double.orderValue_nextUp, JPoints.Double.orderValue_nextDown}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:doubleValue}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item For doubles~$x$ and~$y$ that are no NaN, $x < y$ holds in C exactly when $v(x) < v(y)$.
    \item The value~$\nu(k)$ is strictly increasing in~$k$ on $[-\Omega, \Omega]$.
    \item For a double~$x$ that is no NaN and not~$+\infty$, $\mathrm{nextUp}(x)$ is no NaN and its
      order value is that of~$x$ plus one; for a double~$x$ that is no NaN and not~$-\infty$,
      $\mathrm{nextDown}(x)$ is no NaN and its order value is that of~$x$ minus one.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Double.toRat}
  The value of a double that is no NaN is~$\nu$ of its order value
  (\lname{JPoints.Double.doubleValue\_eq\_valueOfOrderValue}). In units of~$2^{-1074}$, $\nu(k)$ for
  $0 \le k = 2^{52}e + t < \Omega$ is~$t$ for $e = 0$ and $(2^{52} + t)\,2^{e - 1}$ for $e \ge 1$,
  which grows with~$k$ also from one exponent field to the next, $\nu(-k) = -\nu(k)$,
  $\nu(\Omega) = \top$
  and $\nu(-\Omega) = \bot$; so the comparison of the order values is that of the values.
  \texttt{nextafter} adds one to the bits of a double without the sign bit and takes one from those
  of a double with it, but for the zeros, whose neighbors are~$\pm 2^{-1074}$.
\end{proof}

The comparisons \texttt{<=} and \texttt{==} are the order and the equality of the values in the same
way (\lname{JPoints.Double.le\_eq\_true\_iff}, \lname{JPoints.Double.beq\_eq\_true\_iff}).

\section{Roundings}\label{sec:rounding}

Every operation of the program that makes a double from an exact result rounds it as IEEE~754 does
in the rounding mode to nearest with ties to even. The model has two roundings, of a dyadic number
and of a fraction; the operations and the conversions are built on them.

\begin{definition}[Rounding to a double]\label{def:Double.ofDyadic}
  \lean{JPoints.Double.ofDyadic, JPoints.Double.ofRatio}
  \leanok
  For a sign~$\mathrm{neg}$, $m \in \NN$ and $k \in \ZZ$, $\mathrm{ofDyadic}(\mathrm{neg}, m, k)$ is
  the double nearest to~$\pm m\,2^k$, with ties to even: a signed zero for $m = 0$; otherwise, for
  the exponent~$E$ with $2^E \le m\,2^k < 2^{E+1}$, the last place~$2^q$ of the result,
  $q = \max(E - 52, -1074)$, and the integer~$M$ nearest to $m\,2^{k - q}$, carried into the next
  exponent at $M = 2^{53}$, a subnormal or a zero for $M < 2^{52}$, and an infinity beyond the
  largest finite double. For $d \ne 0$, $\mathrm{ofRatio}(\mathrm{neg}, n, d)$ is the double nearest
  to~$\pm n/d$ in the same way, $M$ the integer nearest to $n/(d\,2^q)$.
\end{definition}

The proofs show of these roundings what the theorems need: that they are faithful, and that their
error is that of a rounding to nearest, which the error bounds of the tube and of the real region
need. That the model rounds to the nearest
double with ties to even is checked when the model is compiled, on the cases at the edges (both
zeros, the subnormals, the largest finite doubles, the infinities, the NaNs, ties of the rounding,
$2^{53}$ and~$2^{63}$ with their neighbors), against the bits that a C program built with gcc and
glibc prints.

\begin{lemma}[Faithful rounding]\label{lem:Double.lt_nextUp_and_nextDown_lt_ofRatio}
  \lean{JPoints.Double.lt_nextUp_and_nextDown_lt_ofRatio, JPoints.Double.lt_nextUp_and_nextDown_lt_ofDyadic}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:doubleValue, def:Double.ofDyadic}
  Let $n, d \ne 0$. If $x = \mathrm{ofRatio}(\mathrm{neg}, n, d)$ is finite, then
  $v(\mathrm{nextDown}(x)) < \pm n/d < v(\mathrm{nextUp}(x))$. Likewise, for $m \ne 0$, if
  $x = \mathrm{ofDyadic}(\mathrm{neg}, m, k)$ is finite, then
  $v(\mathrm{nextDown}(x)) < \pm m\,2^k < v(\mathrm{nextUp}(x))$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Double.toRat, lem:Double.lt_eq_true_iff}
  The rounded significand lies within half a unit of the exact quotient by the last place
  (\lname{JPoints.Double.ofRatio\_rounding}, \lname{JPoints.Double.ofDyadic\_rounding}), and the
  neighbors of the result lie farther away: a whole unit, or half a unit below a power of two, from
  which an exact value below it lies within a quarter of a unit.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact conversions]\label{lem:Double.ofDyadic_exact}
  \lean{JPoints.Double.ofDyadic_exact, JPoints.Double.isFinite_ofInt_and_toRat}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Double.toRat, def:Double.ofDyadic}
  If $0 < m < 2^{53}$, $k \ge -1074$ and $\lfloor \log_2 m \rfloor + k \le 1023$, then
  $\mathrm{ofDyadic}(\mathrm{neg}, m, k)$ is finite, its sign bit is~$\mathrm{neg}$ and its value is
  $\pm m\,2^k$. For an integer~$n$ with $|n| < 2^{53}$, the double \texttt{(double)n} is finite with
  the value~$n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Then $q \le k$, and $m\,2^{k - q}$ is an integer below~$2^{53}$, which the rounding keeps. The
  conversion \texttt{(double)n} is $\mathrm{ofDyadic}(n < 0, |n|, 0)$
  (\lname{JPoints.Double.ofInt}).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Roundings to nearest]\label{def:Double.IsRounding}
  \lean{JPoints.Double.IsRounding}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Double.toRat}
  A double~$x$ is a \emph{rounding} of $w \in \QQ$ when~$x$ is no NaN, $x$ is finite if
  $|w| < 2^{1022}$, and a finite~$x$ has $\bar x \ge 0$ if $w \ge 0$, $\bar x \le 0$ if $w \le 0$,
  and
  \[ |\bar x - w| \le |w|/2^{53} + 2^{-1075}\,, \]
  half a unit in the last place of a normal double, or of a subnormal one.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The conversions round to nearest]\label{lem:Double.isRounding_ofDyadic}
  \lean{JPoints.Double.isRounding_ofDyadic, JPoints.Double.isRounding_ofRatio, JPoints.Double.isRounding_ofInt}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Double.ofDyadic, def:Double.IsRounding}
  The double $\mathrm{ofDyadic}(\mathrm{neg}, m, k)$ is a rounding of~$\pm m\,2^k$, for $d \ne 0$
  the double $\mathrm{ofRatio}(\mathrm{neg}, n, d)$ is a rounding of~$\pm n/d$, and for an
  integer~$n$ the double \texttt{(double)n} is a rounding of~$n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Double.toRat}
  The significand lies within half a unit of the exact value, so the error is at most~$2^{q - 1}$:
  at most $|w|/2^{53}$ for $q = E - 52$, as $2^E \le |w|$, and $2^{-1075}$ for $q = -1074$. Below
  $2^{1022}$ the result does not overflow, and the rounding keeps the sign; a zero is exact.
\end{proof}

\section{The arithmetic}\label{sec:doubleArith}

Write $x \oplus y$, $x \ominus y$, $x \otimes y$ and $x \oslash y$ for $x + y$, $x - y$, $x * y$ and
$x / y$ of C on doubles.

\begin{definition}[The arithmetic of the doubles]\label{def:Double.add}
  \lean{JPoints.Double.add, JPoints.Double.sub, JPoints.Double.mul, JPoints.Double.div, JPoints.Double.sqrt}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Double.ofDyadic}
  The four operations of C on doubles and \texttt{sqrt} of \texttt{math.h}, as the scalar
  instructions \texttt{addsd}, \texttt{subsd}, \texttt{mulsd}, \texttt{divsd} and \texttt{sqrtsd}
  of SSE2 compute them: a NaN for a NaN operand, the default NaN for an invalid operation
  ($\infty - \infty$, $0 \cdot \infty$, $0/0$, $\infty/\infty$, the square root of a negative
  number), the infinities and the signs of the zeros of IEEE~754, and otherwise the exact result
  rounded to nearest with ties to even, by~$\mathrm{ofDyadic}$ for $\oplus$ and~$\otimes$ and
  by~$\mathrm{ofRatio}$ for~$\oslash$, an infinity on overflow; $x \ominus y$ is
  $x \oplus (-y)$ but for NaN operands. The square root is rounded from the integer square root of
  the significand, scaled to between $2^{104}$ and~$2^{106}$, the remainder deciding the rounding.
\end{definition}

\formalnote{The model is that of the default build, \texttt{gcc -O2} for x86-64 without
\texttt{-march}: every operation is a scalar instruction of SSE2, and no product is contracted with
a sum into a fused multiply-add. Of two NaN operands the result is the first, quieted; gcc may swap
the operands of~$+$ and~$*$, which changes only the sign and the fraction of such a NaN, on which
nothing in the program depends.}

\begin{lemma}[The operations round to nearest]\label{lem:Double.isRounding_add}
  \lean{JPoints.Double.isRounding_add, JPoints.Double.isRounding_sub, JPoints.Double.isRounding_mul, JPoints.Double.isRounding_div}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Double.toRat, def:Double.IsRounding, def:Double.add}
  For finite doubles~$x$ and~$y$, $x \oplus y$ is a rounding of $\bar x + \bar y$, $x \ominus y$ one
  of $\bar x - \bar y$ and $x \otimes y$ one of $\bar x \bar y$; if $\bar y \ne 0$, $x \oslash y$ is
  a rounding of $\bar x / \bar y$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Double.ofDyadic, def:Double.add, lem:Double.isRounding_ofDyadic}
  The exact sum, difference and product of two finite doubles are dyadic numbers, and their exact
  quotient is a fraction; the operations round them by $\mathrm{ofDyadic}$ and~$\mathrm{ofRatio}$
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:Double.isRounding_ofDyadic}). An exact result~$0$ gives a zero.
\end{proof}

The same library proves what the search reads of three computations in doubles as integers
(Chapter~\ref{chap:words}): $\lfloor\sqrt{H}\rfloor$, as \texttt{floor(sqrt(height))} computes it,
is the integer square root of the height bound
(\lname{JPoints.Double.toInt\_toInt64\_floor\_sqrt\_ofInt}); the number of the full periods of
\texttt{walk\_parts} is the exact one
(\lname{JPoints.Double.toInt\_toInt64\_ofInt\_mul\_one\_div\_add\_half}); and the quotient of
\texttt{row\_setup} by a reciprocal is $\lfloor d/p \rfloor$ or one less
(\lname{JPoints.Double.toInt\_toInt64\_ofInt\_mul\_one\_div\_mem\_Icc}).

\section{Errors counted in roundings}\label{sec:approx}

The analysis of the tube evaluates polynomials in doubles and needs a bound of the error of each
result that holds for every input. A rounding to nearest multiplies by a factor within $2^{-53}$
of~$1$, but for the absolute error $2^{-1075}$ of the subnormal range in
Definition~\ref{def:Double.IsRounding}, which grids remove: every finite double lies on the grid
$2^{-1074}\ZZ$, and the values of a computation whose inputs are integers keep to grids, the grid of
a product the sum of the grids of its factors.

\begin{definition}[Roundings on a grid]\label{def:OnGrid}
  \lean{JPoints.OnGrid, JPoints.Double.IsGridRounding}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Double.toRat}
  A rational number~$w$ lies on the grid $2^{-k}\ZZ$ when $w = n/2^k$ for an integer~$n$. A
  double~$x$ is a \emph{grid rounding} of~$w$ on the grid $2^{-k}\ZZ$ when~$x$ is finite,
  $|\bar x - w| \le |w|/2^{53}$ and $\bar x$ lies on the grid.
\end{definition}

A finite rounding by $\mathrm{ofDyadic}$ of a value on the grid $2^{-k}\ZZ$, $k \le 1074$, is a grid
rounding: either the last place of the result is at most the spacing of the grid, and the rounding
is exact, or it is coarser, and the result is normal
(\lname{JPoints.Double.isGridRounding\_ofDyadic}).

\begin{definition}[Approximations]\label{def:Double.Approx}
  \lean{JPoints.Double.Approx}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Double.toRat, def:OnGrid}
  For $w, a \in \QQ$ and $n, k \in \NN$, a double~$x$ \emph{approximates}~$w$ with the
  majorant~$a$ to within~$n$~roundings on the grid $2^{-k}\ZZ$ when~$x$ is finite, $|w| \le a$,
  \[ |\bar x - w| \le \bigl((1 + 2^{-53})^n - 1\bigr)\,a\,, \]
  and $\bar x$ lies on the grid $2^{-k}\ZZ$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The calculus of approximations]\label{lem:Double.Approx.add}
  \lean{JPoints.Double.Approx.add, JPoints.Double.Approx.mul, JPoints.Double.Approx.neg, JPoints.Double.Approx.fabs}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Double.Approx, def:Double.add}
  Let~$x$ approximate~$w$ with the majorant~$a$ to within~$n$~roundings on the grid $2^{-k}\ZZ$.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item If~$y$ approximates~$w'$ with the majorant~$b$ to within~$m$~roundings on the same grid,
      $k \le 1074$ and $x \oplus y$ is finite, then $x \oplus y$ approximates $w + w'$ with the
      majorant $a + b$ to within $\max(n, m) + 1$ roundings on~$2^{-k}\ZZ$.
    \item If~$y$ approximates~$w'$ with the majorant~$b$ to within~$m$~roundings on the grid
      $2^{-l}\ZZ$, $k + l \le 1074$ and $x \otimes y$ is finite, then $x \otimes y$
      approximates~$w w'$ with the majorant~$ab$ to within $n + m + 1$ roundings
      on~$2^{-(k + l)}\ZZ$.
    \item The double $-x$ approximates~$-w$, and \texttt{fabs(x)} approximates~$|w|$, with the
      majorant~$a$ to within~$n$~roundings on~$2^{-k}\ZZ$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Double.toRat, def:Double.ofDyadic, def:Double.add, def:OnGrid}
  Write $\rho_j = (1 + 2^{-53})^j$ and $j = \max(n, m)$. The exact sum $s = \bar x + \bar y$ lies on
  the grid, so $x \oplus y$ is a grid rounding of~$s$, within $|s|/2^{53}$ of it; with
  $|s - (w + w')| \le (\rho_j - 1)(a + b)$ and $|s| \le \rho_j\,(a + b)$ the errors add up to
  $(\rho_{j+1} - 1)(a + b)$. For the product,
  $\bar x \bar y - w w' = \bar x\,(\bar y - w') + (\bar x - w)\,w'$ is at most
  $(\rho_{n+m} - 1)\,ab$ in absolute value, $|\bar x \bar y| \le \rho_{n+m}\,ab$, and
  $\bar x \bar y$ lies on the grid $2^{-(k+l)}\ZZ$. Negation and \texttt{fabs} change only the sign
  bit.
\end{proof}

So the count of a result is the number of roundings on the longest path to it, and its majorant the
value of the computation on the absolute values of the inputs. The inputs are approximations:
\texttt{(double)n} of an integer below $2^{53}$ in absolute value is exact
(\lname{JPoints.Double.approx\_ofInt}), and \texttt{mpz\_get\_d}, which truncates, approximates an
integer within two roundings on the grid $2^{-53}\ZZ$ (\lname{JPoints.Double.approx\_mpzGetD}). A
finite result has finite operands, which \texttt{tube\_ok} needs (Chapter~\ref{chap:tube}):
\lname{JPoints.Double.isFinite\_and\_of\_isFinite\_add} and
\lname{JPoints.Double.isFinite\_and\_of\_isFinite\_mul}. The bounds become linear in the count:

\begin{lemma}[The error factor of $n$ roundings]\label{lem:one_add_pow_sub_one_le}
  \lean{JPoints.one_add_pow_sub_one_le}
  \leanok
  For $n \le 2^{20}$, $(1 + 2^{-53})^n - 1 \le n\,2^{-53}\,(1 + 2^{-32})$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Write $\rho_n = (1 + 2^{-53})^n$. Bernoulli's inequality in the form
  $\rho_n\,(1 - n\,2^{-53}) \le 1$ gives $\rho_n - 1 \le n\,2^{-53}\rho_n$, and, as
  $n\,2^{-53} \le 1/2$, $\rho_n \le 2$, so that
  $\rho_n - 1 \le 2^{20} \cdot 2^{-53} \cdot 2 = 2^{-32}$; then
  $\rho_n - 1 \le n\,2^{-53}\,\bigl(1 + (\rho_n - 1)\bigr) \le n\,2^{-53}\,(1 + 2^{-32})$.
\end{proof}

\section{The Sturm chain}\label{sec:sturmChain}

The real region of a row (Chapter~\ref{chap:region}) leaves out words by the sign of~$f$: it needs
open intervals on which~$f$ is negative. \texttt{real\_roots\_init} finds them once, for the
polynomial~$f$ of the curve searched, in the exact arithmetic of GMP's rationals but for the ends
of the intervals, which are doubles. It builds the Sturm chain
$p_0 = f$, $p_1 = f'$ and $p_i = -(p_{i-2} \bmod p_{i-1})$ over~$\QQ$ in an array
\texttt{sturm[7][7]} (\csource{j-points.c}{1496}{1497}); it bounds the real roots by a Cauchy
bound~$B$; it isolates them in $(-B, B]$ by bisection, counting them by the sign changes of the
chain; and it records the gaps between the isolating intervals on which~$f$ is negative.

\formalnote{The array \texttt{sturm[7][7]} of \texttt{mpq\_t}s is one flat array of $49$
rationals in the model, and the arrays of the isolating intervals hold six rationals each; they
start at~$0$, as \texttt{mpq\_init} sets them. These statics live only during
\texttt{real\_roots\_init}; the model passes them on as values.}

\begin{definition}[Finished Sturm chains]\label{def:SturmChain.IsFinished}
  \lean{JPoints.rowPoly, JPoints.SturmChain.IsFinished}
  \leanok
  For the flat array~$s$ of a chain,
  $\mathrm{rowPoly}(s, i) = \sum_{k < 7} s_{7i + k} X^k \in \QQ[X]$ is the polynomial of the
  row~$i$. A chain of $\ell$~rows is \emph{well formed} for $f \in \QQ[X]$
  (\lname{JPoints.SturmChain.WF}) when $\ell \ge 2$, the rows $0$ and~$1$ are~$f$ and~$f'$, each row
  is a polynomial of the degree recorded for it, the degrees decrease strictly, the row~$i$ has a
  degree of at most $6 - i$, and each row from the third on is the negated remainder of the two
  before it; it is \emph{finished} when moreover the negated remainder of its last two rows is zero.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The contract of the Sturm chain]\label{lem:realRootsChain_spec}
  \lean{JPoints.realRootsChain_spec}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:SturmChain.IsFinished}
  From a chain that is well formed for $f \in \QQ[X]$, \lname{JPoints.realRootsChain} returns a
  finished chain for~$f$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Each pass of the loop appends the negated remainder of the last two rows unless it is zero; the
  division subtracts multiples of the divisor until the degree is below that of the divisor
  (\lname{JPoints.realRootsReduce}, \lname{JPoints.realRootsReduce\_spec}), and the loop also
  stops at a constant row, by which every remainder is zero. The chain begins with~$f$ and~$f'$
  (\lname{JPoints.realRootsStart\_spec}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The chain is Mathlib's Sturm sequence]\label{lem:SturmChain.IsFinished.map_rowPoly_eq_sturmSeq}
  \lean{JPoints.SturmChain.IsFinished.map_rowPoly_eq_sturmSeq, Polynomial.sturmSeq_map}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:SturmChain.IsFinished}
  The polynomials $\mathrm{rowPoly}(s, i)$, $i < \ell$, of a finished chain for~$f$ are Mathlib's
  Sturm sequence of~$f$ and~$f'$ (\lname{Polynomial.sturmSeq}), the list
  $f, f', -(f \bmod f'), \dots$ of the successive negated remainders, ending at the last nonzero
  one. For a homomorphism $\varphi \colon K \to L$ of fields and $p, q \in K[X]$, the Sturm sequence
  of~$\varphi(p)$ and~$\varphi(q)$ is the image of that of~$p$ and~$q$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The rows are nonzero polynomials, each from the third on the negated remainder of the two before
  it, and the next negated remainder is zero; such a list is the Sturm sequence of its first two
  entries, by the recursion of \lname{Polynomial.sturmSeq}
  (\lname{Polynomial.map\_range\_eq\_sturmSeq}). A homomorphism of fields commutes with remainders
  and negation and keeps a polynomial nonzero, so it commutes with that recursion.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[The sign variations]\label{def:sturmV}
  \lean{JPoints.sturmV}
  \leanok
  For $F \in \mathbb{R}[X]$ and $x \in \mathbb{R}$, $V_F(x)$ is the number of the sign variations
  of the values at~$x$ of the Sturm sequence of~$F$ and~$F'$: the number of the adjacent pairs of
  opposite signs once the zeros are removed (\lname{List.signVariations}).
\end{definition}

Sturm's theorem (Definition~\ref{def:SturmTheorem}) says that for a squarefree $F \in \mathbb{R}[X]$
and $a \le b$ the number of the roots of~$F$ in $(a, b]$ is $V_F(a) - V_F(b)$. The proofs use it in
the following form.

\begin{lemma}[Sturm's theorem from below a bound]\label{lem:ncard_add_sturmV}
  \lean{JPoints.ncard_add_sturmV}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:SturmTheorem, def:sturmV}
  Assume Sturm's theorem. Let $F \in \mathbb{R}[X]$ be squarefree with all its real roots below~$B$.
  Then for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, the number of the roots $y > x$ of~$F$ plus $V_F(B)$
  is~$V_F(x)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  For $x \le B$ this is Sturm's theorem on $(x, B]$, which holds every root above~$x$; for $x > B$,
  Sturm's theorem on $(B, x]$, which holds no root, gives $V_F(x) = V_F(B)$.
\end{proof}

\texttt{sturm\_changes} evaluates the rows at a rational number by Horner's rule, in exact
arithmetic, and counts the changes of sign, the zeros skipped.

\begin{lemma}[The counts of the chain]\label{lem:SturmChain.IsFinished.run_sturmChanges}
  \lean{JPoints.sturmChanges_spec, JPoints.SturmChain.IsFinished.run_sturmChanges}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:SturmChain.IsFinished, def:sturmV}
  For a chain that is well formed for $f \in \QQ[X]$ and $x \in \QQ$, \lname{JPoints.sturmChanges}
  returns the number of the sign variations, zeros skipped, of the values at~$x$ of the polynomials
  of its rows. For a finished chain this count is $V_F(x)$, where $F \in \mathbb{R}[X]$ is the image
  of~$f$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:SturmChain.IsFinished.map_rowPoly_eq_sturmSeq}
  Horner's rule evaluates each row exactly, and the loop over the rows keeps the sign of the last
  nonzero value and the count. The rows of a finished chain are the Sturm sequence of~$f$ and~$f'$
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:SturmChain.IsFinished.map_rowPoly_eq_sturmSeq}), whose image in $\mathbb{R}[X]$ is
  that of~$F$ and~$F'$, and the map $\QQ \to \mathbb{R}$ keeps the values and their signs.
\end{proof}

\section{The isolation of the roots}\label{sec:isolation}

The bound of the roots (\csource{j-points.c}{1605}{1611}) is computed in integers.

\begin{lemma}[The bound of the roots]\label{lem:realRootsBound_spec}
  \lean{JPoints.realRootsBound_spec, Polynomial.cauchyBound_le_add_one}
  \leanok
  The bound \lname{JPoints.realRootsBound} of integer coefficients $f_0, \dots, f_d$ with
  $f_d \ne 0$ is an integer $B \ge 2$ with $|f_k| \le |f_d|\,(B - 2)$ for every $k < d$. A
  polynomial~$p$ over a normed division ring with $\|p_k\| \le \|\mathrm{lc}(p)\|\,C$ for every
  $k < \deg p$ has a Cauchy bound (\lname{Polynomial.cauchyBound}) of at most $C + 1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The bound is $B = 2 + \max_{k < d} \lceil |f_k|/|f_d| \rceil$, the maximum taken from~$0$; the
  Cauchy bound of~$p$ is $1 + \max_{k < \deg p} \|p_k\|/\|\mathrm{lc}(p)\|$.
\end{proof}

Every root has an absolute value below the Cauchy bound
(\lname{Polynomial.IsRoot.norm\_lt\_cauchyBound}), so the real roots of~$f$ lie in $(-B, B)$
(\lname{Polynomial.abs\_lt\_add\_one\_of\_isRoot}). When~$B$ has at most
$\texttt{ROOT\_BITS} = 480$ bits (\csource{j-points.c}{1500}{1502}), \texttt{real\_roots\_init}
isolates them in $(-B, B]$; otherwise it records no interval, and the real region is every row
whole. \texttt{sturm\_isolate} halves an interval $(lo, hi]$ at its midpoint until it holds one
root, by the counts at its ends, and refines that interval (\csource{j-points.c}{1536}{1543}): it
halves it again, keeping the half that holds the root, until the doubles~$l$ and~$h$ of its ends,
which \texttt{mpq\_get\_d} gives, pass the test $h \ominus l \le 2^{-41} \otimes (|l| \oplus |h|
\oplus 1)$ in doubles.

\formalnote{Where the conversions of C round to nearest, GMP's \texttt{mpq\_get\_d} and
\texttt{mpz\_get\_d} truncate towards zero to a double: a value of at least $2^{1024}$ in absolute
value becomes an infinity, and one below $2^{-1074}$ the zero~$0.0$, also for a negative value
(\lname{JPoints.Double.mpqGetD}, \lname{JPoints.Double.ofRatioTrunc}).}

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{mpq\_get\_d} truncates faithfully]\label{lem:Double.lt_nextUp_and_nextDown_lt_mpqGetD}
  \lean{JPoints.Double.lt_nextUp_and_nextDown_lt_mpqGetD}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:doubleValue}
  For every $r \in \QQ$, the double $x = \texttt{mpq\_get\_d}(r)$, $r$ truncated towards zero,
  satisfies $v(\mathrm{nextDown}(x)) < r < v(\mathrm{nextUp}(x))$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Double.toRat, lem:Double.lt_eq_true_iff}
  The truncated significand is the integer part of the exact quotient by the last place, so~$r$
  lies between~$\bar x$ and the next double away from zero, short of it; the zero below $2^{-1074}$
  has the neighbors~$\pm 2^{-1074}$, and an infinity beyond~$2^{1024}$ the largest finite double on
  one side and itself, of the value $\bot$ or~$\top$, on the other.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The refinement test of \texttt{sturm\_isolate}]\label{lem:Double.mpqGetD_sub_mpqGetD_le}
  \lean{JPoints.Double.mpqGetD_sub_mpqGetD_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Double.add, def:Double.ofDyadic}
  Let $-2^{480} \le lo < hi \le 2^{480}$ be rational numbers with $hi - lo \le 2^{-43}$, and let
  $l = \texttt{mpq\_get\_d}(lo)$ and $h = \texttt{mpq\_get\_d}(hi)$. Then the test of the refinement
  holds in doubles:
  $h \ominus l \le (0.5 \otimes \texttt{ldexp}(1.0, -40)) \otimes (|l| \oplus |h| \oplus 1.0)$,
  where $|\cdot|$ is \texttt{fabs} and $\le$ the comparison of C.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Double.toRat, def:doubleValue, lem:Double.lt_eq_true_iff, def:Double.IsRounding, lem:Double.isRounding_add}
  By \lname{JPoints.Double.isFinite\_mpqGetD\_and\_abs\_toRat\_sub\_le}, the truncations move the
  ends by at most $2^{-52}$ times their absolute values plus~$2^{-1074}$, and the operations are
  roundings (Lemma~\ref{lem:Double.isRounding_add}), each within $2^{-53}$ of its exact result but
  for~$2^{-1075}$; for a width of at most $2^{-43}$ the left side stays below the right.
\end{proof}

Write $N(x)$ for the count of \lname{JPoints.sturmChanges} at $x \in \QQ$ for a chain.

\begin{definition}[Isolating intervals]\label{def:RootIntervals.Isolated}
  \lean{JPoints.RootIntervals.Isolated}
  \leanok
  Let the arrays of \lname{JPoints.RootIntervals} hold the intervals $(lo_j, hi_j]$, $j < n$. They
  are \emph{recorded up to}~$x$ for the chain and the count~$v_0$ when the arrays have six entries,
  $n + N(x) = v_0$, and $N(lo_j) + j = v_0$ and $N(hi_j) + j + 1 = v_0$ for every $j < n$.
\end{definition}

With~$v_0$ the count at the lower end of the whole interval, and under Sturm's theorem, the
number~$n$ of the intervals is then the number of the roots up to~$x$, and the interval~$j$ holds
the root~$j$ and no other, in increasing order.

\begin{lemma}[The contract of \texttt{sturm\_isolate}]\label{lem:sturmIsolate_spec}
  \lean{JPoints.sturmRefine_spec, JPoints.sturmIsolate_spec}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:RootIntervals.Isolated, def:Double.add, def:Double.ofDyadic}
  Let the counts~$N$ of a chain decrease on $[lo_0, hi_0]$, by at most six from~$lo_0$ to~$hi_0$,
  with $-2^{480} \le lo_0$ and $hi_0 \le 2^{480}$ (\lname{JPoints.SturmChain.Counts}), and let
  $lo_0 \le lo < hi \le hi_0$.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item If $N(lo) = n$ and $N(hi) + 1 = n$, the refinement \lname{JPoints.sturmRefine} returns an
      interval $(lo', hi']$ with $N(lo') = n$ and $N(hi') + 1 = n$.
    \item If the intervals given are recorded up to~$lo$ for the count~$N(lo_0)$, and the counts
      passed are $N(lo)$ and~$N(hi)$ (\lname{JPoints.IsolatePre}), then the intervals that
      \lname{JPoints.sturmIsolate} returns, when its fuel lasts, are recorded up to~$hi$ for the
      count~$N(lo_0)$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:Double.mpqGetD_sub_mpqGetD_le}
  The refinement halves $(lo, hi]$ at its midpoint and keeps the half whose counts at the ends
  differ by one, which one of them does, as the counts decrease; the width halves at each step, and
  once it is at most $2^{-43}$ the test holds (Lemma~\ref{lem:Double.mpqGetD_sub_mpqGetD_le}), so
  the loop ends. The isolation goes by induction on the fuel: nothing for $N(lo) = N(hi)$; for
  $N(lo) = N(hi) + 1$ the refined interval, recorded with its counts; and otherwise the lower half
  isolated, then the upper half from the intervals recorded up to the midpoint. The decrease by at
  most six keeps the intervals within the six entries of the arrays.
\end{proof}

\formalnote{The recursion of \texttt{sturm\_isolate} ends in the C code because the counts are those
of Sturm's theorem and the roots of the squarefree~$f$ are distinct. The model gives it a fuel, the
depth that it may still descend (\lname{JPoints.sturmIsolateFuel}), and records no interval when the
fuel runs out; Mahler's bound on the distance of two roots of a squarefree integer polynomial makes
the fuel sufficient, as the docstring of \lname{JPoints.sturmIsolateFuel} argues. The theorems do
not depend on it: no interval is a sound answer.}

\section{The intervals on which \texorpdfstring{$f$}{f} is negative}\label{sec:negativeIntervals}

Between the isolating intervals of the roots $i - 1$ and~$i$ of the~$n$~roots lies a gap, from
$-\infty$ for $i = 0$ and to~$+\infty$ for $i = n$.

\begin{lemma}[The gaps between the isolating intervals]\label{lem:not_isRoot_and_ncard_eq}
  \lean{JPoints.not_isRoot_and_ncard_eq}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:SturmTheorem, def:sturmV}
  Assume Sturm's theorem. Let $F \in \mathbb{R}[X]$ be squarefree with all its real roots in
  $(-B, B)$ and $n + V_F(B) = V_F(-B)$, and let $lo_j, hi_j \in \mathbb{R}$, $j < n$, satisfy
  $V_F(lo_j) + j = V_F(-B)$ and $V_F(hi_j) + j + 1 = V_F(-B)$. Let $i \le n$ and $x \in \mathbb{R}$
  with $hi_{i-1} < x$ if $i > 0$ and $x < lo_i$ if $i < n$. Then~$x$ is no root of~$F$, and exactly
  $n - i$ roots of~$F$ lie above~$x$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:ncard_add_sturmV}
  By Lemma~\ref{lem:ncard_add_sturmV}, all~$n$~roots lie above~$-B$, $n - i$ above~$lo_i$ and
  $n - i$ above~$hi_{i-1}$. The number of the roots above~$x$ lies between these, and a root at~$x$
  would make one more above~$hi_{i-1}$ than above~$x$.
\end{proof}

A squarefree real polynomial has, at a point that is no root, the sign of its leading coefficient
times $(-1)$ to the number of the roots above the point
(\lname{Polynomial.sign\_eval\_eq\_sign\_leadingCoeff\_mul\_of\_squarefree}). So in the gap~$i$, $f$
has the sign of~$f_d$ for an even $n - i$ and the opposite one otherwise, which is the sign that
the record (\csource{j-points.c}{1622}{1641}) computes for the gap. For a gap of negative sign it
records, as doubles, the gap grown by the isolating intervals at its ends, rounded outwards, and
the gap shrunk by them, rounded inwards: the shrunk gap runs from
$\mathrm{nextUp}(\texttt{mpq\_get\_d}(hi_{i-1}))$ to $\mathrm{nextDown}(\texttt{mpq\_get\_d}(lo_i))$,
with $-\texttt{HUGE\_VAL}$ and \texttt{HUGE\_VAL} for the ends at $-\infty$ and~$+\infty$. The real
region reads the shrunk intervals; the grown ones are written and never read.

\begin{definition}[Sound negative intervals]\label{def:NegativeIntervals.Sound}
  \lean{JPoints.NegativeIntervals.Sound}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:poly, def:doubleValue}
  The intervals \lname{JPoints.NegativeIntervals}, in the globals \texttt{num\_neg},
  \texttt{neg\_shrunk\_lo}, \texttt{neg\_shrunk\_hi} and those of the grown ones, are \emph{sound}
  for the coefficients~$cs$ when there are at most four, the ends~$l_i$ and~$u_i$ of the shrunk ones
  are no NaN, and $\mathrm{poly}(cs)$ is negative at every rational number~$x$ with
  $v(l_i) < x < v(u_i)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The contract of the record of the negative intervals]\label{lem:realRootsRecord_spec}
  \lean{JPoints.realRootsRecord_spec}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:poly, def:NegativeIntervals.Sound}
  Let the polynomial~$f$ of the coefficients $f_0, \dots, f_d$ have $f_d \ne 0$, let there be at
  most six isolating intervals, and let~$f$ be negative at every rational~$x$ of the gap~$i$,
  $hi_{i-1} < x < lo_i$ (a condition at a missing end dropped), whenever the sign that the record
  computes for that gap, that of~$f_d$ for an even $n - i$ and the opposite one otherwise, is
  negative (\lname{JPoints.RecordPre}). Then \lname{JPoints.realRootsRecord} returns intervals that
  are sound for the coefficients of~$f$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:doubleValue, lem:Double.lt_eq_true_iff, lem:Double.lt_nextUp_and_nextDown_lt_mpqGetD}
  The signs of the at most seven gaps alternate, so that at most four are negative. The shrunk ends
  are $\pm\texttt{HUGE\_VAL}$ or neighbors of truncations, no NaN; and by
  Lemma~\ref{lem:Double.lt_nextUp_and_nextDown_lt_mpqGetD},
  $hi_{i-1} < v(\mathrm{nextUp}(\texttt{mpq\_get\_d}(hi_{i-1})))$ and
  $v(\mathrm{nextDown}(\texttt{mpq\_get\_d}(lo_i))) < lo_i$, so a rational number between the values
  of the shrunk ends lies in the gap.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[\texttt{real\_roots\_init}]\label{def:realRootsInit}
  \lean{JPoints.realRootsInit}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Double.add, def:Double.ofDyadic}
  \lname{JPoints.realRootsInit} is \texttt{real\_roots\_init} for the seven coefficients of the
  polynomial~$f$ of the curve searched, of degree~$d$. It builds the Sturm chain of~$f$ and~$f'$
  and the bound~$B$ of the roots. When~$B$ has at most \texttt{ROOT\_BITS} bits, it isolates the
  roots in $(-B, B]$ and records the gaps of negative sign, grown and shrunk, as
  \lname{JPoints.NegativeIntervals}; otherwise, and when the fuel of the isolation runs out, it
  returns no interval.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The contract of \texttt{real\_roots\_init}]\label{lem:realRootsInit_spec}
  \lean{JPoints.realRootsInit_spec}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:poly, def:SturmTheorem, def:realRootsInit, def:NegativeIntervals.Sound}
  Assume Sturm's theorem. For seven integer coefficients~$cs$ of degree $d \ge 5$ whose polynomial
  $\mathrm{poly}(cs)$ is squarefree, \lname{JPoints.realRootsInit} returns intervals that are sound
  for~$cs$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:doubleValue, def:SturmChain.IsFinished, lem:realRootsChain_spec, def:sturmV, lem:ncard_add_sturmV, lem:SturmChain.IsFinished.run_sturmChanges, def:RootIntervals.Isolated, lem:sturmIsolate_spec, lem:not_isRoot_and_ncard_eq, lem:realRootsBound_spec, lem:realRootsRecord_spec}
  The chain is finished (Lemma~\ref{lem:realRootsChain_spec}), so that its counts are~$V_F$ for the
  image~$F$ of~$f$ in $\mathbb{R}[X]$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:SturmChain.IsFinished.run_sturmChanges}). The
  roots of~$F$ lie in $(-B, B)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:realRootsBound_spec}), and $B < 2^{480}$ when it
  has at most $480$~bits; so the counts decrease on $[-B, B]$ by the number of the roots, at most
  six, by Lemma~\ref{lem:ncard_add_sturmV} (\lname{JPoints.SturmChain.IsFinished.counts}). The
  isolation records the roots up to~$B$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sturmIsolate_spec}), and by
  Lemma~\ref{lem:not_isRoot_and_ncard_eq} and the sign of a squarefree polynomial,~$f$ has in each
  gap the sign that the record computes (\lname{JPoints.RootIntervals.Isolated.recordPre});
  Lemma~\ref{lem:realRootsRecord_spec} concludes. No interval at all is sound.
\end{proof}

The real region uses these intervals through their soundness: $f$ is negative at every real point
inside a sound interval (Lemma~\ref{lem:NegativeIntervals.Sound.eval_map_neg_of_contains}), and the
polynomial of the curve searched, to which \texttt{real\_roots\_init} is applied, is squarefree
(Lemma~\ref{lem:squarefree_poly_searchedCoeffs}).
